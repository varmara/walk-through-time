South of the river: Test cricket, Nottingham Forest and the county's headquarters.

## Trent Bridge cricket ground
- source: en:Trent Bridge
- group: trent-bridge
- see: The first Test, 1899 | The Trent Bridge Inn
- check: 1838 | Trent Bridge Inn | William Clarke | 1841 | 1899

The first recorded match was played on ground behind the Trent Bridge Inn in 1838, and William Clarke opened
the ground in 1841. It hosted its first Test match, England against Australia, in 1899.

## County Hall

Nottinghamshire County Council's headquarters, designed by Vincent Harris. Building paused during the Second
World War, and it was only completed in 1954.

## City Ground
- source: en:City Ground
- group: trent-bridge
- check: 1898 | seventh home | city status | three hundred yards

Nottingham Forest moved here in 1898, to their seventh home. The name dates from 1897, when Nottingham was
granted city status, and Notts County's Meadow Lane is only three hundred yards away, on the other side of the
Trent.

## St Giles' Church
- source: en:St Giles Church, West Bridgford
- group: west-bridgford
- at: name:St Giles'

A medieval church of which little remains. Thomas Chambers Hine restored it in 1872, and in 1896 a new nave
and chancel were begun, their foundation stone laid by Lady Byron of Thrumpton Hall.

The settlement began in the early 10th century, when defences and houses were built at the south end of Trent

## Won at cards
- source: en:West Bridgford
- group: west-bridgford
- at: pin:-1.1274,52.9320
- names: no
- note: a pin in the town centre: the story is about the whole estate, not one building
- check: 1523 | game of cards | 1894 | 1972 | Rushcliffe

The Musters family gained the West Bridgford estate in 1523, reputedly by winning it in a game of cards. When
they sold land for building they set strict rules for it, and the village grew into a suburb, with its own
council from 1894 and part of the borough of Rushcliffe from 1972.

## The first out-of-town superstore
- source: en:West Bridgford
- group: west-bridgford
- at: osm:way/80847199
- names: no
- check: 1964 | out-of-town superstore | 1966 | Asda | 1999

In 1964 an American company opened what was called the UK's first major out-of-town superstore on Loughborough
Road. In 1966 the young Asda chain took it over, and in 1999 replaced it with a much larger store on the land
next to it.

## Edwalton station
- source: en:Edwalton railway station
- group: edwalton
- see: Lady Bay Bridge
- at: pin:article
- names: no
- note: a pin at the article's point: the station is gone, and the houses on its site are not it
- check: 1880 | 1941 | Melton Mowbray | Old Dalby | Advanced Passenger Train

The station opened in 1880 on the Midland Railway's direct line from Nottingham to Melton Mowbray, which let its
London expresses avoid Leicester. It closed to passengers in 1941, and its site is now housing. Beyond it the
line became the Old Dalby test track, where the Advanced Passenger Train was tried.

## Lady Bay Bridge

## The Lady Bay bombs
- source: en:Lady Bay, Nottinghamshire
- at: pin:article
- group: west-bridgford
- names: no
- check: 1941 | Luftwaffe | Pinders Ponds | bomb sites
- note: a pin at the article's point, in Lady Bay: it does not say where the bombs fell

In 1941 a German bomber dropped a line of bombs across Lady Bay. New houses went up on the bomb sites in the 1950s
and 1960s, and Pinders Ponds, to the east, are thought to be flooded bomb craters.
