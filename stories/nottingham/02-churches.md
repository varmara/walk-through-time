# Churches
id: churches

Medieval Nottingham had three parishes: St Mary's, St Peter's and St Nicholas'. Each still has its church,
joined in the 1840s by a Catholic cathedral, and by chapels of the Nonconformists, such as the Unitarians on
High Pavement.

One of the three medieval parish churches of Nottingham, on a site mentioned in the Domesday Book.

## St Nicholas' Church
- source: en:St Nicholas' Church, Nottingham
- year: 1678
- check: Civil War | 1678

The medieval church was pulled down during the Civil War; the brick church standing today was finished in
1678.

The Catholic cathedral, designed by Augustus Pugin and built between 1841 and 1844.

## High Pavement Chapel
- source: en:High Pavement Chapel
- at: name:Former Unitarian Chapel
- year: 1876
- check: 1876 | Stuart Colman | 1690 | 1802 | 1982 | Pitcher

Built in 1876 to designs by Stuart Colman for a congregation that goes back to 1690 and became Unitarian in
1802. Worship ended in 1982; the chapel then housed a lace museum and is now a Pitcher & Piano.
