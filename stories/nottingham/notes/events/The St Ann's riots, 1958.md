On 23 August 1958, at closing time, a fight outside a pub in St Ann's grew into a riot between white and
West Indian residents; the police restored order within 90 minutes. Some 4,500 people took part in the disturbances
to some extent, and a crowd of about 4,000 gathered the following weekend. The Chief Constable, Athelstan Popkess,
played the events down afterwards and said they were not about race. With
the Notting Hill riots a week later, the disturbances led to talks between Caribbean governments and the British
government.
