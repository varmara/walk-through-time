After a poor harvest in 1766, food was dear and people feared shortages. At Goose Fair that October, when
Lincolnshire merchants began carting away cheese they had bought, the townspeople tried to stop them; the fair turned
to a riot, shops, a warehouse and a boat were looted, and wheels of cheese were rolled down the streets. The mayor
could not restore order, and soldiers fired on the crowd, killing William Eggleston, a farmer guarding his own goods.
It took several days to bring the town back to order.
