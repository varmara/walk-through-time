Magnetoencephalography (MEG) maps the brain at work from the tiny magnetic fields its cells make, but its scanners
weighed about half a tonne, because their sensors had to be kept at −269 °C, and the patient had to keep still. In
March 2018 researchers at the Sir Peter Mansfield Imaging Centre, with UCL, showed in Nature a scanner worn like a
helmet, built from new "quantum" sensors in a 3D-printed shell.[^uon] Wearing it, people could nod, stretch, drink
tea and even play ping pong while their brains were scanned.[^ucl]
