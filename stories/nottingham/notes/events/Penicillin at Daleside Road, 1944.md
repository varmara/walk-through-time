In the Second World War the Ministry of Supply asked Boots to design and run a plant that would make penicillin in
quantity, by growing the mould on the surface of its broth.[^boots] Boots built it on Daleside Road, and made huge
amounts of penicillin there during the war.[^basin] It was the largest surface-culture plant in the country,[^grace]
or by another account in the world,[^gateway] and one of the first penicillin factories built anywhere.[^adams] Its
monthly production reports run from August 1944.[^wellcome] In 1945 a newly qualified pharmacist, Stewart Adams,
later the leader of the team that found ibuprofen, took his first job there.[^adams]
