The Representation of the People Act, passed on 6 February 1918, gave the vote to women of 30 and over who met a
property qualification, and they voted for the first time on 14 December 1918. That November women had also been
allowed to stand for Parliament: in Nottinghamshire the first to do so was Violet Markham, at Mansfield, who lost.
In 1919 Bridgid Burke-Bloor became the first woman to stand for Nottingham City Council, and in 1920 [[Helena
Dowson]] and Caroline Harper were its first women councillors. Women won the vote on the same terms as men on 2 July
1928. A hundred years to the day after that first vote, the city unveiled a plaque by a juniper tree in the
Arboretum for the suffragette [[Helen Watts]].[^tree]
