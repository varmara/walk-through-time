Statues and memorials show who a town chose to honour, and when. Nottingham's range from a Chartist leader and Victorian MPs to the city's war dead and, in the 20th and 21st centuries, figures of legend and football, and at last the women of its past.

## Victorian heroes, 1859–1905
Nottingham's first public park, the Arboretum, opened on 11 May 1852 on land set aside under the town's Inclosure Act of 1845.[^arb] It became the town's first home for statues: a Chartist MP and the memorial of a county regiment.

## Between the wars, 1921–1929
After the First World War thousands of memorials went up across Britain and its empire, many of them modelled on the Cenotaph in Whitehall, unveiled on 11 November 1920.[^cenotaph] In Nottingham the war dead came first; then a new Council House gave the Market Square its stone lions.
- 1921: [[Statue of Captain Albert Ball]]
- 1927: [[City War Memorial]]
- [[Memorial to Samuel Morley]]
- [[Council House]]

## Legend and football, 1952–2008
After the Second World War the city stopped raising statues to its MPs and soldiers, and turned to the figures it was known for around the world: a legendary outlaw and a football manager.
- 1952: [[Statue of Robin Hood]]
- 2008: [[Statue of Brian Clough]]
