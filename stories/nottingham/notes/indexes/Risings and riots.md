Nottingham was known for popular protest. Food riots, machine-breaking, political riots and the Chartist campaigns all left their mark, as working people reacted to hunger, falling wages and their exclusion from political power. The St Ann's riots of 1958 had different causes.

## The cheese riot, 1766
After a poor harvest food was dear, and when merchants began carting cheese away from Goose Fair, the townspeople tried to stop them. The fair turned to a riot that took several days to put down.

## The Luddites, 1811
Frame-breakers went after the "wide" knitting frames used to make cheap goods. The movement began in Arnold in March 1811 and spread across Nottinghamshire.

## The Pentrich rising, 1817
In June 1817 men from Derbyshire marched on Nottingham, led by Jeremiah Brandreth, the Nottingham Captain. The news that set them marching was a hoax by a government informer, and the rising ended at Giltbrook.

- [[The Pentrich rising, 1817]]

## The Reform riots, 1831
When the House of Lords threw out the Second Reform Bill in October 1831, rioting broke out in Nottingham. In the days that followed crowds burned the castle, sacked Colwick Hall and burned a silk mill in Beeston; three men were hanged.

## The Chartists, 1842
In August 1842 Nottingham's workers stopped work for it, and troops broke up their meetings, ending with mass arrests on Mapperley Hills. Five years later the town elected the Chartist leader as its MP.

## The Bogs, 1872
In Bulwell the wet meadow by the Leen was common land. When the lord of the manor tried to enclose it, the townspeople marched on it to keep it.
- [[The Bogs]]

## The St Ann's riots, 1958
The last of these disturbances had different causes. In August 1958 a fight in St Ann's grew into a riot between white and West Indian residents, a week before the Notting Hill riots in London.
