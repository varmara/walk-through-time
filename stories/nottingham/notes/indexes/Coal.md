Britain's coal output peaked in 1913, at 287 million tonnes;[^peak] its last deep mines closed in 2015.[^end] Nottinghamshire's pits, and the wagonways, canals, railways and power stations that served them, lie between those dates.

## Wagons and canals, 1603–1839
The great age of canal building in England ran from the 1790s to the 1810s. The first canals were paid for by merchants, makers and mine owners who needed to ship their goods.[^canal]

## Deep pits and ironworks, 1861–1899
Coal employed more people in Britain than ever before or since in 1920, when 1,191,000 worked in it.[^men]

## Coal for power, 1925–2024
From 1926 a Central Electricity Board built Britain's first national grid, finished in September 1933. Later the biggest power stations were built on the coalfields themselves, such as West Burton, in the Nottinghamshire coalfield near the Trent.[^grid]

## The last pits, 1947–1999
On 1 January 1947 most mines passed to the new National Coal Board, and in the 1950s and 1960s closing pits became routine.[^ncb] After the strike of 1984–85 the Nottinghamshire miners split from the National Union of Mineworkers and formed a union of their own, the Union of Democratic Mineworkers, in December 1985.[^udm]
