Much of historic Nottingham has gone. Some buildings were cleared for road schemes and shopping centres in the 20th century; others were destroyed in riots or simply outlived their purpose. These stories record what stood on sites that look very different today.

## Lost villages, 1276–1779
England has more than 3,000 deserted medieval villages. Many were once thought to have emptied when the Black Death killed their people, but the population was already falling by the early 14th century, and most villages seem to have been abandoned in the 15th; later, enclosure turned fields into sheep pasture and emptied hundreds more.[^dmv]

## The monks driven out, 1538
Between 1536 and 1541 Henry VIII dissolved the monasteries, priories, convents and friaries of England, Wales and Ireland, seized their wealth and sold their property. Thomas Cromwell ran the work, and in 1536 the Pilgrimage of Grace rose against it in Yorkshire and Lincolnshire.[^diss]
- [[Lenton Priory]]

## Riot and railway, 1829–1904
Riots burned the castle and a mill in 1831. Later in the century the railways took more: Victoria station alone, opened in 1900, needed a 13-acre site and the demolition of some 1,300 houses and 24 pubs.[^vic]

## Making way, 1926–1960
Between the wars and after the Second World War, old buildings gave way to a new Council House, new roads and new parks, and a village station lost its passengers.

## The great clearances, 1965–1975
In these years the city centre gained two shopping centres. The Victoria Centre was built from 1967 to 1972 on the site of Victoria station, keeping only its clock tower and hotel, and its 26 floors of flats are still the city's tallest building.[^vc] The Broad Marsh centre followed in 1975.

## Gone in our time, 2020–2022
Demolition of the Broad Marsh centre began in October 2019. The pandemic stopped the work in March 2020, and its owner, Intu, collapsed that June; in August the council announced it would demolish the rest, at a cost of £8 million.[^bm]
