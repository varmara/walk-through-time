Freemasons have met in Nottingham since the 1750s: first in the town's inns, later in a hall of their own on Goldsmith Street, which is still the home of the county's lodges.

## Lodges in the inns, 1755–1844
The first Grand Lodge was formed in London in 1717. In 1751 six lodges set up a rival Grand Lodge, the Antients, and the two were not united until 1813, as the United Grand Lodge of England.[^antients]

## Dukes and foundation stones, 1863–1898
Nottinghamshire's Freemasons were led by dukes. The 6th Duke of Newcastle was installed as Provincial Grand Master at the Mechanics' Hall in Nottingham in January 1866.[^newcastle] In 1863 there were only four lodges in the town: in order of seniority, the Newstead, the Royal Sussex, the Commercial and the Pelham.[^portland]
- [[The School of Art's stone, 1863]]

## A hall of their own, 1880–1930
Freemasonry grew fast around the First World War: between 1910 and 1928 the number of Freemasons in the world doubled, to about 4,400,000.[^cl]
