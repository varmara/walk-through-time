The war grew out of a long struggle between the monarchy and Parliament for political and religious control.[^ecw] Nottingham was where it began, and a Parliament town through all of it, with Royalist garrisons round it.

## The King's standard, 1642
That year Parliament claimed command of the county militias, and Charles answered with commissions of his own. When the fighting began in August, both sides believed one battle would settle it.[^militia]

## Holding the castle, 1643–1645
The enemy was close. Newark, where the Great North Road met the Fosse Way and crossed the Trent, was a Royalist stronghold, besieged three times; it held out until the King ordered it to surrender in May 1646.[^newark]

## Shelford and the end of the castle, 1645–1651
When the war ended, Parliament made many castles useless, slighting them so that they could not be held against it again.[^slight]
