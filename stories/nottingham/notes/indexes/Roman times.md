Before the Romans this was the land of the Corieltauvi, a Celtic tribe of the East Midlands whose capital was at Leicester, and there is little sign that they fought the conquest.[^cor] No Roman town stood where Nottingham is now: the traces of those centuries lie around it.

## The army, 1st century AD
For the first few decades after the invasion of AD 43, the Fosse Way, which crossed this county, marked the western frontier of Roman rule.[^fosse]

## Farms and villas, 1st–4th centuries
The Romans brought better farming, and by the 4th century lowland Britain was prosperous enough to send grain across the sea. That wealth paid for a blossoming of villa building and decoration between AD 300 and 350.[^rb]

## By the rivers, Roman times
Roman builders paved fords with flagstones on timber piles, as at Littleborough further down the Trent, where a Roman road crossed the river to the town of Segelocum.[^trent]
