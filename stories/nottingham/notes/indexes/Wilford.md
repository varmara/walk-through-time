Wilford is a village on the south bank of the Trent, linked to Nottingham for centuries by ferry and, from 1870, by a toll bridge. Its church, manor and inn record a long rural history; in the late 19th century it became a suburb.

## Ford, manor and church
Wilford's history begins at the river: remains of a Roman ford were found here in 1900. The manor is recorded in Domesday Book, and the church of St Wilfrid was founded in the 14th century.

## The village and its big house
From the 17th to the early 19th century Wilford was a quiet riverside village, centred on its inn and, from 1781, on Wilford House. Among its residents were a sea captain, a poet and the leader of the Pentrich rising.

## Bridges, coal and the suburb
From the late 1860s Wilford changed rapidly. A colliery was opened, a toll bridge replaced the ferry in 1870, and the population almost doubled in the 1890s. In 1925 a power station was built on the opposite bank.

## Sportsmen of Wilford
Wilford produced several notable footballers in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, among them Harry Cursham of Notts County and two Iremongers.

## War and the trams
The churchyard holds the graves of servicemen from both World Wars. In 2015 the tram reached Wilford, linking the village to the city again.
