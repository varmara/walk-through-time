Art Nouveau, the style of curving lines and plant forms that was at its height around 1900, never shaped Nottingham's large buildings, which stayed Gothic or Baroque. It arrived in the details instead: in shopfronts and the tiles of a shop's side wall, in a station's iron gates, and in the porches of ordinary terraced houses.

## Shops and a station, 1896–1904
Art Nouveau was most popular between 1890 and 1910, and drew on the curves of plants and flowers. In Britain it was also called the Modern Style, and London's Liberty store did much to spread it through its floral fabrics.[^an]

## Lace money in Long Eaton, 1901–1906
The railways brought Long Eaton its growth, and lace mills went up, Harrington Mill of 1885 among them. By 1907 the town had almost 1,400 lace machines and over 4,000 people, a quarter of its population, worked in lace.[^le]

## On the doorstep, 1900–1914
Sneinton's population rose from 17,439 in 1891 to its peak of 23,093 in 1901. Land was sold to developers, and the houses they built on the slopes of Sneinton Dale were of a higher standard than the streets before them.[^sn]
