Long before its factories, Nottingham's ground gave it stone, clay and iron. From the later 19th century the town was known as much for what it made as for lace: medicines, beer, clothes, bicycles and cigarettes. Boots, Raleigh and Player's became household names and among the city's largest employers.

## Stone, clay and iron
The rock and clay under the town were worked long before its famous firms: limestone for building at Bulwell, clay for pots and bricks in St Ann's, Mapperley and Carlton, and iron at Stanton on the county's edge.

## Boots
Boots began with a herbalist's shop on Goose Gate in 1849 and grew into a national firm. Jesse and Florence Boot built up the firm and gave generously to Nottingham. In the 1930s it built factories now listed Grade I, was bombed in the Blitz, and in 1961 its chemists made ibuprofen.

## Beer, sauce and soap
The 19th century brought breweries and works making goods for the household. Shipstones in New Basford and the Home Brewery in Arnold supplied the city's pubs for over a century, and HP Sauce began in a pickle and sauce factory in Basford.

## Clothes
Clothing was a long-standing trade in Nottingham, grown out of the stocking frame. In the 19th century hosiery firms such as I. & R. Morley employed thousands. A century later Paul Smith, from Beeston, opened a small shop on Byard Lane and built an international fashion business from it.

## Wheels and engines
From the 1870s Nottingham's workshops made bicycles. Thomas Humber moved his works to Beeston, and a small workshop in Raleigh Street grew, under Frank Bowden, into a company that covered acres of Faraday Road. George Brough built motorcycles called the Rolls-Royce of their kind, and from 1927 Rolls-Royce itself tested aero engines at Hucknall.

## Tobacco
John Player's Castle Tobacco Factories made Radford one of the centres of the British cigarette trade. The bonded warehouses where the firm kept its tobacco were among the last of its buildings to go up, in 1939, and tobacco making in the city lasted until 2016.
