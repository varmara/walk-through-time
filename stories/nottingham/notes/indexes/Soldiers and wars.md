Nottingham sent soldiers and sailors to wars across the centuries and raised its own county regiment. The two World Wars also reached the city itself, through the munitions factories and depots, the losses at Chilwell and the bombing of 1941.

## A French marshal on parole, 1705–1711
The War of the Spanish Succession set Britain and its allies against France. At Blenheim on 13 August 1704 the Duke of Marlborough and Eugene of Savoy destroyed a French and Bavarian army; more than 14,000 French soldiers were taken prisoner, among them their commander.[^blen]

## At sea, 1710–1822
Admiral Sir John Borlase Warren, born at Stapleford, sat as MP for Nottingham from 1797 while still at sea. In October 1798 his squadron, off Donegal, did much to defeat a French fleet sent to invade Ireland. He died in 1822, and has a monument in St Mary's Church, Attenborough.[^warren]

## The county regiments, 1779–1908
From 1782 regiments of foot were given county names, and Nottinghamshire had two: the 45th, and the 59th, raised in 1755, which became the 2nd Nottinghamshire. The 59th fought in China in the Second Opium War and in Afghanistan in 1878–1880. Under the Childers reforms of 1881 it joined the East Lancashire Regiment.[^59th]

## War work, 1914–1918
In 1915 a shortage of artillery shells at the front became a political crisis, and Lloyd George was made Minister of Munitions, at the head of a new government department.[^shells] New factories were built to fill shells, Chilwell's among them.

## Remembering, 1921–1927
After the war thousands of memorials went up across Britain and its empire. The national one, the Cenotaph in Whitehall, was unveiled by George V on 11 November 1920, and many towns modelled theirs on it.[^cenotaph]

## Getting ready, 1939–1942
Just before the Second World War the RAF built a system of air defence: radar reports went to a filter room, which sifted them into single plots before fighters were sent up. Its fighters were divided into groups, and 12 Group, covering the Midlands, was based at Hucknall and later at Watnall.[^dowding]

## Bombs and losses, 1941–1943
The Blitz, the German bombing of British cities, ran from 7 September 1940 to 16 May 1941; more than 40,000 civilians were killed by bombing during the war.[^blitz] Nottingham's worst night came in its last fortnight.
