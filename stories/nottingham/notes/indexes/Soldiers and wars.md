Nottingham sent soldiers and sailors to wars across the centuries and raised its own county regiment. The two World Wars also reached the city itself, through the munitions factories and depots, the losses at Chilwell and the bombing of 1941.

## Soldiers and sailors
Before the 20th century the county's links with war came mainly through individual soldiers and sailors and through its regiment. The 45th Foot took the name of Nottinghamshire in 1779 and later became the Sherwood Foresters. Veterans of Victorian wars were given almshouses at Wilford in 1908.

## The First World War
The First World War brought war work to Nottinghamshire on a large scale. The National Shell Filling Factory at Chilwell filled shells for the front, and in July 1918 an explosion there killed 134 people. After the war the city built memorials to its dead.

## The Second World War
In the Second World War the area held military depots and airfields, and the city was a target for German bombers. The worst raid came on the night of 8–9 May 1941, when the city centre and the Boots works were hit.
