The countryside around Nottingham was dominated for centuries by landed families: the Cliftons, Pierreponts, Willoughbys and others. Their houses and estates shaped the villages around them, and many were sold, divided or put to new uses in the 20th century.

## Medieval lords, 1257–c. 1500
After 1066 all land in England belonged to the king, who granted its use in return for service. The manor was the basic unit of landholding: its lord had a manor house and demesne land for his own use, and the Domesday Book of 1086 recorded the manors one by one.[^manor]

## Tudor and Stuart houses, 1523–1677
Between about 1570 and 1620 courtiers and the very rich built "prodigy houses", large and showy, many of them to receive Elizabeth I and her court on their progresses around the country. Wollaton is one of them.[^prodigy]

## Georgian country houses, 1700s–1790s
Country house building reached its height from the late 17th to the mid-18th century. A house was often designed by one eminent architect in the most fashionable style of the day, and some were built to dominate the land around them.[^country]

## Victorian estates, 1843–1874
After the Industrial Revolution newly rich industrialists and bankers were eager to show their wealth and taste, and by the 1850s their new mansions were built in one of the many revival styles.[^country]

## Sold, given away or pulled down, 1933–1965
In the 20th century about one in six of England's country houses was pulled down, some 1,200 since 1900. Two world wars took their servants, and taxes and death duties, raised to new heights after 1945, made them too costly to keep; by 1955 one was being demolished every five days.[^lost]
