The Old Market Square was the centre of Nottingham for centuries. It lay where the town's Saxon and Norman settlements met, and was for long the site of the town's markets, fairs and government. The surrounding streets hold some of the city's oldest inns and many of its theatres and halls.

## The market
The great Market Place was where the town traded, held Goose Fair and gathered for public events, from the cheese riot of 1766 to parliamentary elections. The Exchange, built in the 1720s, housed the Corporation, and its replacement, the Council House of 1929, still dominates the square.

## Houses, hotels and halls
As the town grew richer in the 18th and 19th centuries, the streets around the square filled with substantial buildings: a Georgian town house that became a subscription library, theatres, a hotel and music halls. Several have been put to new uses, as Rock City shows.
