A library and a hospital paid for by subscribers, a miller who taught himself mathematics, engineers who brought clean water, women who fought their way into medicine, and two discoveries that went round the world.

## Almshouses and hospitals, 1241–1392
By 1241 Nottingham's Hospital of St John the Baptist had a rule of life for its brothers and sisters, laid down by Walter de Grey, Archbishop of York;[^orb] it stood at the corner of today's Parliament Street and Glasshouse Street.[^nhh]
- [[Plumptre Hospital]]

## Learning and healing, 1752–1821
Hospitals of this time were voluntary: they were paid for by subscriptions, gifts and fundraising. By the middle of the 18th century there were six in London.[^vol]
- [[Bromley House]]
- [[Nottingham General Hospital]]

## Self-taught science, 1793–1880s
From 1821 mechanics' institutes taught working men science and technical subjects, with libraries and apparatus for experiments; by the middle of the century there were over 700 in Britain and abroad.[^mech]
- [[George Green]]
- [[Green's Windmill]]
- [[Edward Joseph Lowe]]

## Clean water, 1831–1884
Cholera reached Britain in October 1831. The epidemic led in time to the Public Health Act of 1848.[^cholera]
- [[Thomas Hawksley]]
- [[Charles Blacker Vignoles]]
- [[Marriott Ogle Tarbotton]]

## Hospitals and asylums, 1857–1880
The Lunacy Act of 1845 made the mentally ill patients, many of whom had been held in gaol as prisoners, and the County Asylums Act of the same year built up a public network of asylums.[^lunacy]
- [[Coppice Hospital]]
- [[Mary Potter]]
- [[Mapperley Hospital]]

## A university college, 1877–1928
The university began as an adult school founded in 1798 and lectures given from 1873 by the University of Cambridge in other towns; University College Nottingham was founded in 1881.[^univ]
- [[University College]]
- [[Trent Building]]

## Women in medicine, 1899–1930s
From 11 August 1876 the Medical Act let every British medical authority license qualified applicants, women as well as men.[^1876]
- [[Sarah Gray]]
- [[Margaret Glen-Bott]]
- [[Louisa May Long]]

## Discoveries, 1944–2018
Alexander Fleming found penicillin in 1928; Howard Florey and Ernst Chain's team at Oxford made it into a medicine, and the three shared the Nobel Prize in 1945. During the war it became an important part of the Allied effort, saving thousands of lives.[^pen]
