For three centuries Nottingham lived by its textiles. The hosiery trade settled in the town after 1730, and with its offshoot, lace, it came to dominate the town's economy through the 18th and 19th centuries.[^hist]

## Wool and the frame, 1285–1728
Wool was one of the most important parts of medieval England's economy, and English wool was prized across Europe.[^wool]

## Cotton mills, 1768–1791
In 1769 Richard Arkwright patented a spinning frame that twisted cotton into a strong yarn, fit for the warp threads that had been spun by hand, and it greatly reduced the cost of spinning.[^ark]

## Hard times, 1811–1831
The years 1810 to 1812 brought high unemployment and inflation, made worse by the cost of the wars with Napoleon, to a trade in which work was already notoriously precarious.[^hard]

## Lace, 1813–1907
In 1808 John Heathcoat built a machine that made an exact imitation of pillow lace, a net called bobbinet, and patented it in 1809. In 1816 men believed to be paid by Nottingham lacemakers broke into his factory at Loughborough and destroyed 55 of its lace frames.[^heath]

## Lace remembered, 1969–2009
Lace declined as technology changed: by the 1970s fewer than 5,000 people worked in the Lace Market, and many of its factories stood derelict. In 1969 it became the city's first conservation area, and its warehouses have since found new uses.[^lm]
