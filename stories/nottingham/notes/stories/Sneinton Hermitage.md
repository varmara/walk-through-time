Cave dwellings dug into the sandstone ridge extended out into Sneinton, where they were known as the Hermitage. When
the road below was made, houses with brick fronts were built against the rock, the caves serving as their back rooms,
among them two pubs, the White Swan and the Earl Manvers' Arms. On 10 May 1829 an overhanging rock fell, crushing two
houses and the dancing room cut in the rock.[^mellors] Some of the caves were lived in until 1867.[^caves] In 1888
the London and North Western Railway opened a goods station on top of the rock, lowering it by 25 feet and pulling
down the rock houses in the way of its bridge.[^sm] In 1897 the Great Northern's high-level line pushed the road some
60 feet north, and most of the houses and caves to the west went; in 1904 those east of the railway bridge were
blasted or pulled down.[^mellors] What little remains can still be seen along Sneinton Hermitage.
