In 1392 John de Plumptre, a Mayor of Nottingham, founded a hospital here for "thirteen poor women broken down of age
and depressed of poverty", with two priests. Huntingdon Plumptre repaired it in 1650, and the present building was
begun when its first stone was laid on 1 August 1823. The charity closed in 1991, when it could no longer afford to
improve the building, and in 2001 the Royal National Institute for the Blind took it over.
