When the Duke of St Albans became Nottinghamshire's Provincial Grand Master in 1878, the brethren raised the idea of
a hall of their own. It was built here on Goldsmith Street and opened in 1880, as a meeting place for the lodges and a
Masonic club.[^fcn] Lodges that had met in inns moved in from 1881.[^lane] The present hall, of brick with stone
details in the Neo-Classical style usual for Masonic halls, was designed in 1928 by John Howitt & Son, one of the last
works of John Howitt, himself a Freemason; the city lists it as a local heritage asset.[^insight] The first lodge
consecrated in it was Temple Lodge, on 30 October 1930.[^temple]
