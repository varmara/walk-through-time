In 1643 Colonel John Hutchinson took command of Nottingham Castle, and Parliament later made him governor of both
town and castle. It was a hard post: the town had no walls, the garrison was small and short of supplies, and the
committee that ran the town quarrelled. Hutchinson held on all the same, and protected the radical separatists among
his soldiers as far as he could.
