A local account says Sir William Clifton planted the avenue of elms in 1677, from a little stone bridge over Fairham
Brook up to the hall; it was said to be wide enough for six carriages abreast.[^walk] In 1853 it was "an avenue of
trees a mile in length".[^white] By the 18th century the Grove was a favourite outing, and an 1874 guide tells of
thousands of Nottingham artisans there at Easter and Whitsuntide.[^walk] The Nottingham poet [[Henry Kirke White]], a
butcher's son, published Clifton Grove, a Sketch in Verse, in 1803, aged 17. D. H. Lawrence set a scene of Sons and
Lovers (1913) in the Grove.[^walk]
