The medieval bridge had a chapel of St James at one end and was kept by a religious house, until on 21 February 1551
a royal charter passed its repair to the Corporation, with lands to pay for it, the Bridge Estate. Floods damaged it
again and again, and in 1683 they swept away its northern half. Rebuilt with fifteen arches, 538 ft long in all, it
stood until its foundations grew unsafe, and in the 1860s a new bridge was begun.
