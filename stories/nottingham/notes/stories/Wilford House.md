The house was designed in 1781 for the Smith family as Wilford Hall, the centre of an estate with stables, lodges for
the house staff and a private chapel.[^walk] It is a red-brick country house of three storeys and five bays, listed
Grade II, and its stable block of the same year is listed too. The Smiths' bank in Nottingham is believed to have been
the first bank outside London, and Samuel Smith, an MP for 44 years, is believed to have built the house; by 1790 it
was his.[^throsby] The banker Abel Smith died at Wilford on 12 July 1788. His wife's younger sister, Elizabeth Bird,
was the mother of William Wilberforce, the slavery abolitionist, who stayed at the house for several months between
1786 and 1789. In 1853 it was the seat of Henry Smith, a large, handsome brick mansion; he owned about 70 acres of the
parish, and in 1828 he built an infant school in the village.[^white]

Thomas Forman, who founded the Nottingham Post, later bought the house from the Smiths, and it passed to the Forman
Hardy family; at some point it was renamed Wilford House. It became offices in the 1980s, the Wilford Place estate
was built on its grounds in 2006,[^trail] and in 2021 the house was given a multi-million pound refurbishment.
