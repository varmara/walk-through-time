Henry II gave Lenton Priory an eight-day fair for St Martin's Day, which Henry III lengthened to twelve, with tolls
paid to the monks for every booth. The fair outlived the priory: in 1584, when servants of Mary, Queen of Scots came,
it was a horse fair, and by the 17th century it was known as a great fair for all sorts of horses. In the 19th
century farmers and horse dealers still came.
