A Roman villa of the winged-corridor kind stood at Glebe Farm, lived in from the 1st to the 4th century.

In 1858 a mosaic floor about 15 feet by 10 was found under the soil here, and charred wood suggested the house had
burned.[^pc] The Castle Museum turned the floor down as a gift in 1878, as moving it would have cost a few pounds.[^pc] It was
later lifted and taken to the rectory, and has since been lost.[^pc]
