Courts sat on this spot from at least 1375, and prisoners were held here from at least 1449. The hall was rebuilt to
a design by [[James Gandon]] in 1769–72, and largely rebuilt again after a fire in 1876. In 1864 Richard Thomas Parker
was hanged on a scaffold over the front steps, the last public execution here. Courts sat here until 1991, and in 1995
the building opened as a museum, now the National Justice Museum.
