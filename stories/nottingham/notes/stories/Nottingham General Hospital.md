A legacy from the banker John Key paid for a hospital on part of Nottingham Park. Its first building, designed by John
Simpson, was begun on 12 February 1781 and opened with 44 beds in September 1782; John Wesley was an early visitor. In
1844 it took in the hurt from a hanging on High Pavement, where twelve died on Garner's Hill: Wikipedia says a stand
collapsed, but the newspapers of the day describe the crowd being forced down the steps into Garner's Hill and
trampled.[^crush] Hine added a storey in
1855, and Alfred Waterhouse's round Jubilee Wing opened in 1900. It closed in 1992, its work moved to the Queen's
Medical Centre.
