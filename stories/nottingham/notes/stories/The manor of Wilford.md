Before the Conquest the manor of Wilford belonged to Gode the Countess.[^throsby] In Domesday Book the settlement is
named Willesforde, with a fishery, a priest and 23 sokemen. Clifton and Wilford, Peverel lands, passed to the Crown
early in Henry II's reign, and the king granted them to Gerbode de Escalt; in Richard I's time they passed to the de
Rodes family. Before 1280 Gervase de Clifton obtained both manors from Sir Gerard de Rodes for £30 a year, and a
charter of 1280 confirmed the grant.[^wood] In 1853 Sir J. G. J. Clifton was lord of the manor, owned all its land but
the church's glebe, and was patron of the church;[^white] in 1870–72 the manor still belonged to Sir R. J.
Clifton.[^gazetteer]
