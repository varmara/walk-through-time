# Barton in Fabis and Thrumpton
id: barton
groups: barton-fabis = Barton in Fabis | thrumpton = Thrumpton

Two villages on the south bank of the Trent, upstream from Clifton: Barton, once Barton in the Beans, and
Thrumpton, with its Jacobean hall.

## Barton in the Beans
- source: en:Barton in Fabis
- group: barton-fabis
- at: pin:-1.2231,52.8901
- names: no
- note: a pin in the village: the story is about the village, not one house
- check: Barton in the Beans | 1774 | Attenborough | Fairham

The village's older name was Barton in the Beans, after the beans grown here; fabis is Latin for beans. A ferry
crossed the Trent from here to Attenborough from before 1774. Today Fairham, a new district, is being built in
the parish beside Clifton.

## Thrumpton Hall
- source: en:Thrumpton Hall
- group: thrumpton
- see: The staircase | The Byrons of Thrumpton | Owners since 1949
- check: Babington | priest hole | Pigot | 1617

The Powdrells, a Roman Catholic family, lost their house here after the Babington Plot; part of it survives
inside the hall, with a priest hole. The Pigots' rose-brick house around it was largely complete by 1617.

- names: no

The hall's Jacobean cantilevered staircase was carved from the estate's own timber, under John Webb, a pupil
of Inigo Jones.

## The Byrons of Thrumpton
- source: en:Thrumpton Hall
- group: thrumpton
- names: no
- check: 1843 | Lucy Wescomb | 1917 | Squarson

The hall came to the Byrons when George Byron, 8th Baron Byron, married Lucy Wescomb in 1843, and they kept it
for about a hundred years. Frederick, the 10th Baron, was the village's vicar and from 1917 its squire as well:
its "squarson".
