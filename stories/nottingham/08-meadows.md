# The Meadows and the river
id: meadows

The low ground between the city and the Trent, with two football grounds facing each other across the river.

## Trent Bridge
- source: en:Trent Bridge (bridge)
- year: 1868..1871
- check: Tarbotton | 1156 | stone arches

The iron bridge was designed by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton and built between 1868 and 1871. A medieval bridge of
more than 20 stone arches, begun in 1156, crossed the river nearby.

## Meadow Lane
- source: en:Meadow Lane
- check: 3 September 1910 | closest

Notts County moved here on 3 September 1910. Divided by the Trent, Meadow Lane and the City Ground are the
closest pair of football grounds in England.

## City War Memorial
- source: en:City War Memorial, Nottingham
- at: name:Memorial Arch
- year: 1927
- check: Thomas Wallis Gordon | Jesse Boot | 11 November 1927

Designed by the City Engineer Thomas Wallis Gordon, on land beside the Trent given by Sir Jesse Boot, and
unveiled on 11 November 1927. It remembers the men of Nottingham who died in both World Wars.

## Victoria Embankment
- source: en:The Meadows, Nottingham
- at: pin:-1.1468,52.9338
- check: 1898 | 1901 | flood defence | Arthur Brown | Memorial Gardens

A mile and a quarter of flood defences along the Trent, built between 1898 and 1901 under the borough engineer
Arthur Brown. Part of it was later laid out as Memorial Gardens to the dead of the First World War.

## The Meadows rebuilt
- source: en:The Meadows, Nottingham
- at: pin:-1.1530,52.9390
- check: pre-1919 | modern council housing | Great Central | 1975

Much of the Meadows' pre-1919 terraced housing was demolished to make way for a new development of modern
council housing, and the viaduct carrying the Great Central railway came down in 1975.
