# Rushcliffe
id: rushcliffe
groups: ruddington = Ruddington | holme-pierrepont = Holme Pierrepont | civil-war = The Civil War | gotham = Gotham | tollerton = Tollerton | rushcliffe-coal = Coal and power

The borough south of the Trent: villages of stocking knitters, a Tudor hall and the gravel pits that became a
centre for watersports, a Civil War massacre and the regicide who led it, the village of fools, an airfield, a
mining village, and the last coal-fired power station in Britain.

## Framework Knitters' Museum

Ruddington's framework knitters worked at hand frames in workshops behind their cottages. Workshops of about
1840 and workers' cottages of about 1829, all listed, now make up the Framework Knitters' Museum.

## Ruddington Depot

- group: ruddington
- at: osm:way/328346132

Built from 1940 to fill and store ammunition, the depot was closed in 1983. Its land is now a transport heritage
centre, a business park and Rushcliffe Country Park.

## The lost church of Flawford

St Peter's, the old church of Flawford, closed in 1718 and was pulled down in 1779. Three alabaster figures found
in it, probably hidden around 1539, are now at Nottingham Castle Museum, and Ruddington's chapel took its name.

Built around 1500, one of the few brick houses of its time in Nottinghamshire. The Pierreponts held Holme from
about 1280 and became Earls and Dukes of Kingston, then Earls Manvers. It is listed Grade I.

## The storming of Shelford House
- source: en:Storming of Shelford House
- at: pin:article
- group: civil-war
- names: no
- check: 1645 | Poyntz | Hutchinson | Philip Stanhope | 160
- see: Colonel Hutchinson | The castle burned, 1831
- note: a pin at the article's point

In November 1645 Parliament's troops under Sydnam Poyntz, with Colonel John Hutchinson leading the assault,
stormed Shelford House, held for the King by Philip Stanhope. About 160 of its defenders were killed, and the
house was burnt that night.

## Colonel Hutchinson
- source: en:John Hutchinson (Roundhead)
- place: en:Owthorpe
- at: pin:article
- group: civil-war
- names: no
- check: Owthorpe | 1615 | 1643 | death warrant | 1664 | Memoirs
- see: The storming of Shelford House | culture/Nottingham Castle
- note: a pin in Owthorpe, at its article's point: his hall there is gone

John Hutchinson, born at Owthorpe in 1615, held Nottingham Castle for Parliament from 1643 and signed Charles I's
death warrant. He died a prisoner in 1664 and is buried at Owthorpe; his wife Lucy's Memoirs of his life made
him famous.

## The Wise Men of Gotham

- group: gotham
- names: no

- note: a pin in the village, at its article's point

A legend says that the villagers played the fool, even trying to fence in a cuckoo, so that King John would build
his road and hunting lodge elsewhere. In 1807 Washington Irving called New York "Gotham", and Batman's Gotham City
took the name.

## Tollerton airfield
- source: en:Nottingham (Tollerton) Airport
- at: osm:way/80257902
- group: tollerton
- names: no
- check: 1929 | 1930 | Sefton Brancker | Lancaster | pillboxes
- note: placed on one of the airfield's listed wartime pillboxes

Licensed in 1929 and opened in 1930 by Sir Sefton Brancker, it was one of Britain's first municipal aerodromes.
In the Second World War it repaired Lancaster bombers; its 18 concrete pillboxes are all listed.

The colliery came in the early 1960s: from 1962 hundreds of mining families moved here from Radford's closed pit,
and the village grew several times over. The pit worked through the 1984–85 strike and
closed in 1993.

## Ratcliffe-on-Soar Power Station
- source: en:Ratcliffe-on-Soar Power Station
- group: rushcliffe-coal
- year: 1968
- check: 1968 | 2024 | 142 years | cooling towers
- see: Wilford Power Station

Generating from 1968, with eight cooling towers, it was the last coal-fired power station in Britain. When it
shut in September 2024 it ended 142 years of coal power in the country.
