# The industrial revolution
id: industrial

How Nottingham and its coalfield helped make the industrial age: an early railway of wood, the first mill of the
factory system, the canal and the railway that carried the coal, the lace machine, the frame-breakers who fought
the machines, and the iron and steam that outlasted the pits.

## Wollaton Wagonway
- source: en:Wollaton Wagonway
- at: pin:-1.2558,52.9727
- names: no
- check: Huntingdon Beaumont | Percival Willoughby | 1604 | Strelley | Wollaton Lane End
- note: a pin in Strelley, where the mines it served were; nothing of the wagonway survives

In 1603–1604 Huntingdon Beaumont and Sir Percival Willoughby laid a wooden wagonway from the Strelley coal pits to
Wollaton Lane End, its wagons drawn by horses. It is described as the first railway in Britain.

## Arkwright's first mill

- note: a pin in the old town: the article does not say where in Nottingham the mill stood

Richard Arkwright's first cotton mill opened in Nottingham in 1768, its machines turned by horses. By 1772 it had
four storeys and 300 workers.

The Luddite movement began in Arnold on 11 March 1811. Across Nottinghamshire the frame-breakers went after the
"wide" knitting frames used to make cheap goods.

## The Leavers lace machine
- source: en:John Leavers
- at: osm:way/100199195
- names: no
- check: Leavers | 1813 | Canning Circus | Heathcoat | Grand-Couronne | 1821

John Leavers lived here, at Canning Circus, where a plaque calls him the inventor of the Leavers lace machine, in
1813. He improved John Heathcoat's lace machine, and in 1821 emigrated to Grand-Couronne in Normandy.

## The Nottingham Canal
- source: en:Nottingham Canal
- at: osm:way/16536642
- names: no
- check: 1792 | William Jessop | 1796 | Langley Mill | Castle Wharf | 1937
- note: placed on Canal House at Castle Wharf, the old warehouses by the canal in the city centre

Authorised in 1792 and engineered by William Jessop, the canal opened in 1796 from the Trent to Langley Mill,
bringing coal to the town. Railways took its trade; most of the main line was abandoned in 1937.

## The railway that began in a pub
- source: en:Midland Counties Railway
- at: osm:way/327309145
- names: no
- check: Sun Inn | Eastwood | 1832 | Mansfield and Pinxton | 1839 | Carrington Street

In 1832 coal owners meeting at the Sun Inn, Eastwood, proposed extending the Mansfield and Pinxton Railway to
Leicester. It became the Midland Counties Railway, whose line from Nottingham, at Carrington Street, to Derby opened
in 1839.

During the Reform riots of 1831, Lowe's Silk Mill in Beeston was burnt on 11 October, the day after Nottingham
Castle, and the same day the riots ceased.

## Bestwood winding engine
- source: en:Bestwood Country Park
- at: osm:way/181053828
- year: 1873
- check: 1873 | vertical steam engine | Bestwood Coal and Iron Company | 1967

The winding engine house of Bestwood Colliery, worked by the Bestwood Coal and Iron Company, keeps its vertical steam
engine of 1873, which lowered the miners and wound up the coal. The pit closed in 1967.

## Bennerley Viaduct
- source: en:Bennerley Viaduct
- at: osm:way/584872269
- year: 1877
- check: wrought iron | 1877 | 1968 | 2022 | Meldon

Built in 1876–1877 of wrought iron, light enough for ground undermined by coal workings. It closed in 1968, was
too costly to take down, and reopened to walkers and cyclists in 2022; only Meldon Viaduct in Devon is like it.
