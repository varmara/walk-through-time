# Science and industry
id: science
groups: science-inventions = Inventions and discoveries | science-works = Water, coal and munitions

Where Nottingham invented, made and mined: from the stocking frame to the bicycle, the medicines of Boots, the
scanner that sees inside the body, and the water, coal and shells the city depended on.

## The stocking frame
- source: en:Stocking frame

In 1589 William Lee of Calverton invented the stocking frame, a machine that knitted. Legend has it that Elizabeth I
refused him a patent. The trade grew here, and from 1728 Nottingham was one of its main centres.

## Raleigh Street

- names: no

- note: a pin on Raleigh Street, where the workshop was; the article does not give its number

In 1885 a small bicycle workshop opened in Raleigh Street, and it gave the company its name. Frank Bowden took
it over in 1888, and by 1897 Raleigh had a purpose-built works of seven and a half acres on Faraday Road.

## Building D10
- source: en:Boots Factory Site
- group: science-inventions
- at: osm:way/37080666
- check: Owen Williams | 1930 | 1932 | Grade I | Wets

Built in 1930–1932 to make Boots' liquid medicines, hence its nickname, the "Wets". Designed by the engineer Owen
Williams, it is listed Grade I, the largest Grade I-listed structure in Britain.

## Building D6
- source: en:Boots Factory Site

- check: Owen Williams | 1935 | 1938 | Drys

Owen Williams's second building for Boots, of 1935–1938, made tablets and lozenges, hence its nickname, the
"Drys". It too is listed Grade I.

## Ibuprofen
- source: en:Stewart Adams (chemist)
- group: science-inventions
- see: Building D10
- at: osm:way/242637128
- names: no
- check: 1961 | Pennyfoot Street | BioCity | 2013 | blue plaque

Ibuprofen was first made at Boots in Nottingham in December 1961, by Stewart Adams's team. Their later laboratory,
on Pennyfoot Street, is now BioCity, and in 2013 a Royal Society of Chemistry blue plaque marked the discovery.

## The MRI scanner
- source: en:Peter Mansfield
- group: science-inventions
- at: osm:way/23303664
- names: no
- check: 1964 | 1978 | 2003 | Paul Lauterbur
- note: on the University's imaging centre named after him

Peter Mansfield came to the University of Nottingham in 1964 and developed magnetic resonance imaging here. Just
before Christmas 1978 a full-body scanner was installed, and he made the first scan of a live patient. He shared
the Nobel Prize in 2003 with Paul Lauterbur.

## Papplewick Pumping Station
- source: en:Papplewick Pumping Station
- group: science-works
- see: Thomas Hawksley
- check: 1881 | 1884 | Marriott Ogle Tarbotton | James Watt | 1969 | 1976

Built in 1881–1884 to pump drinking water for Nottingham from deep wells, with two beam engines by James Watt & Co.
and an engine house by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton, ornate with cast iron and stained glass. It pumped until 1969 and
opened as a museum in 1976.

## Thomas Hawksley
- source: en:Thomas Hawksley
- group: science-works
- see: Papplewick Pumping Station
- at: osm:way/200941630
- names: no
- check: Arnot Hill House | 1807 | Trent Bridge waterworks | 1831 | constant supply

The water engineer Thomas Hawksley was born at Arnot Hill House in 1807. His Trent Bridge waterworks, completed
in 1831, gave Britain its first high-pressure constant supply, which kept dirty water out of the mains.
