The village

The old village on the hill above the Trent: its 12th-century church, the Cliftons' hall, thatched houses, almshouses, a dovecote and the school.

St Mary's Church

c. 1190

The church dates from about 1190, and is listed Grade I. Lewis Nockalls Cottingham restored it in 1846, and much of the decoration is by George Frederick Bodley.

Clifton Hall

13th century

The Clifton family held the manor of Clifton from the late 13th century, and this was their manor house. Sir Gervase Clifton largely rebuilt it between 1778 and 1797, partly to designs by John Carr of York. In 1958 the family sold it, ending 700 years of ownership; it became a girls' grammar school, and is now two houses. The hall itself dates from the late 16th century and is listed Grade I. In its grounds are a brick ice house of the mid-18th century, the vaulted remains of a curved garden stairway, a pair of Coade stone lions of the early 19th century, and the late 18th-century stable block, now cottages.

The thatched houses of Village Road

1319

56 Village Road began in 1319 as a timber-framed farmhouse, later enlarged and cased in brick; it keeps its thatched roof and much of its old timber inside, and is listed Grade II*. Its neighbour, 29 Village Road, is a timber-framed house of about 1500, also thatched and later made into two cottages.

George Wells Almshouses

1709

Their datestone reads "George Wells, Anno 1709", though Wells's will was written in 1712. The almshouses were for six poor unmarried or widowed women, kept up by his lands in Cropwell Bishop, Wilford and Ruddington, with Sir Gervase Clifton and his heirs as trustees; each woman had an allowance and three tons of coal a year from Clifton Colliery. Restored in the 1970s, they are now two homes.

Clifton dovecote

18th century

The red-brick dovecote on the green was built in the early 18th century for the Clifton family's farming estate. It is said to be one of the largest in England, with about 2,300 nesting places; pigeons were kept for winter meat. A stone tablet on its side remembers the men of the village who died in the First World War. It is listed Grade II.

Clifton village school

1871

Henry Robert Clifton laid the school's cornerstone on 3 November 1871, and it opened in April 1872 with 48 children; a night school for men began a year later. It closed in 1956, when new schools were built on the estate, and is now the village hall.

Clifton Grove

1677

A local account says Sir William Clifton planted the avenue of elms in 1677, from a little stone bridge over Fairham Brook up to the hall; it was said to be wide enough for six carriages abreast. In 1853 it was "an avenue of trees a mile in length". By the 18th century the Grove was a favourite outing, and an 1874 guide tells of thousands of Nottingham artisans there at Easter and Whitsuntide. The Nottingham poet Henry Kirke White, a butcher's son, published Clifton Grove, a Sketch in Verse, in 1803, aged 17. D. H. Lawrence set a scene of Sons and Lovers (1913) in the Grove.

The legend of Clifton Grove

A ballad, Bateman's Tragedy, tells of a young man, Bateman, and a beautiful heiress of Clifton who pledge to marry; she breaks her word for a richer widower, and on her wedding day he hangs himself at her door. His ghost haunts her, and in the end carries her off. In the version told locally, the jilted lover drowns himself in the Trent and the bride is dragged into the river at the chasm in the Grove; in another, a Clifton beauty is murdered and thrown down the cliff.

Clifton Bridge

1958

In 1958 a bridge opened here to take traffic off Trent Bridge. It was then the longest pre-stressed concrete bridge in the country, and a second bridge beside it, opened in 1972, made the crossing a dual carriageway.

The estate and its people

Beside the old village the council built one of its largest estates from the 1950s, and two sporting names came from here.

Clifton estate

The old village had 381 people in 1801 and 350 in 1911. Planned from 1950, the Clifton council estate was at one time the largest council estate in Europe. Since the 1980s most of its houses have passed into private ownership.

Viv Anderson

Clifton estate

Vivian Alexander Anderson is an English former professional footballer who played as a right-back. He is best known for his ten-year stint with Nottingham Forest, with whom he won the First Division title in 1978, as well as the European Cup in 1979 and 1980. In 1978, he became the first black footballer to play for England's senior men's national team, and from 1980 to 1988, was selected in the England squad for four major tournaments.

Torvill and Dean

National Ice Centre, Clifton estate

Torvill and Dean are British ice dancers and former British, European, Olympic, and World champions. At the Sarajevo 1984 Winter Olympics the pair won gold and became the highest-scoring figure skaters of all time for a single programme, receiving twelve perfect 6.0s and six 5.9s which included artistic impression scores of 6.0 from every judge, after skating to Maurice Ravel's Boléro.
