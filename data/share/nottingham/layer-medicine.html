George Green

Bromley House, Green's Windmill

George Green was a British mathematical physicist born in Sneinton, Nottinghamshire, now part of the city of Nottingham. Despite receiving little recognition during his lifetime, he is best remembered for his Essay on electricity and magnetism published in 1828.

Wikipedia: George Green (mathematician)

The mathematician George Green was a member of the subscription library at Bromley House.

Wikipedia: George Green (mathematician)

The mathematician George Green was a miller; his family's windmill in Sneinton is restored and named after him.

Thomas Hawksley

1866

The water engineer Thomas Hawksley was born at Arnot Hill House in 1807. His Trent Bridge waterworks, completed in 1831, gave Britain its first high-pressure constant supply, which kept dirty water out of the mains.

Edward Joseph Lowe

Plaque to Edward Joseph Lowe

Edward Joseph Lowe FRS FGS FRAS FLS was a British botanist, meteorologist and astronomer, who published papers on a wide variety of subjects, including luminous meteors, sunspots, the zodiacal light, meteorological observations during the 1860 solar eclipse, conchology, ferns, grasses and other plants.

Wikipedia: Edward Joseph Lowe

Plaque by Southern Broxtowe Blue Plaque Group: “Edward Joseph Lowe FRS 1825-1900. Astronomer, botanist, a founder member of the Meteorological Society, built Broadgate House as his residence and observatory.”

Papplewick Pumping Station

Built in 1881–1884 to pump drinking water for Nottingham from deep wells, with two beam engines by James Watt & Co. and an engine house by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton, ornate with cast iron and stained glass. It pumped until 1969 and opened as a museum in 1976.

Mary Kendrick

Curzon Street

Mary Patricia Kendrick MBE born Mary Patricia Boak was a British tidal engineer who was an expert on silt. She worked on many projects but she is known for leading a team working on the Thames Barrier. She broke a 200 year long list of Admirals who looked after keeping the River Mersey navigable when she appointed Acting Conservator of the River Mersey - a role that dates back to 1625.

Wikipedia: Mary Kendrick

Mary Kendrick, the tidal engineer who led the studies of river levels and silt for the Thames Barrier, grew up on Curzon Street, where her father was a haulage contractor. She later kept the Mersey navigable as its Acting Conservator.

The MRI scanner

Peter Mansfield came to the University of Nottingham in 1964 and developed magnetic resonance imaging here. Just before Christmas 1978 a full-body scanner was installed, and he made the first scan of a live patient. He shared the Nobel Prize in 2003 with Paul Lauterbur.

Margaret Glen-Bott

15 Regent Street

Margaret Glen-Bott (1891–1969) came to Nottingham in 1916. The first woman gynaecologist to be Honorary Surgeon at the Women's Hospital and the Children's Hospital, and later a magistrate, she lived at 15 Regent Street.

Sarah Gray

21 Regent Street

Sarah Gray, Nottingham's first woman GP, lived and practised at 21 Regent Street. At the Women's Hospital from 1899 she was refused charge of in-patients; in 1921 she became the first president of the Nottingham Medico-Chirurgical Society.

Ibuprofen

Ibuprofen was first made at Boots in Nottingham in December 1961, by Stewart Adams's team. Their later laboratory, on Pennyfoot Street, is now BioCity, and in 2013 a Royal Society of Chemistry blue plaque marked the discovery.
