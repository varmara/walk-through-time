Ruddington

A village of framework knitters south of the city, the Second World War ammunition depot beyond it, the site of Flawford's lost church, whose name the village church took, and a Victorian banker's hall.

The lost church of Flawford

St Peter's, the old church of Flawford, closed in 1718 and was pulled down in 1779. Three alabaster figures found in it, probably hidden around 1539, are now at Nottingham Castle Museum, and Ruddington's chapel took its name.

St Peter's Church

c. 1292

Around 1292–94 a manor chapel, St Mary's, was founded in the village. It took the name of St Peter's from the old parish church at Flawford when that was pulled down in the 1770s.

Framework Knitters' Museum

c. 1829

In about 1829 cottages were built for Ruddington's framework knitters, who worked at hand frames in workshops behind them; the workshops date from about 1840. All are listed, and together they now make up the Framework Knitters' Museum.

Ruddington Hall

1860

In 1860 Thomas Cross, a banker from Bolton, built the hall. From 1880 it was the home of Philo Laos Mills, an American merchant who became High Sheriff of Nottinghamshire. In the Second World War it became a hospital, and it stayed one until 1980.

Ruddington Depot

1899

Ruddington's station on the Great Central Railway opened on 15 March 1899. From 1940 a depot was built here to fill and store ammunition, and it closed in 1983. The station had closed in 1963, and the Nottingham Heritage Railway now runs trains on the line. The rest of the depot's land is a business park and Rushcliffe Country Park.
