The Trent Bridge Inn

1838

The inn stood by the bridge before any cricket was played here. The cricketer William Clarke married its landlady, Mary Chapman, and in 1838 they had the field behind it cleared and fenced for the first match on what became Trent Bridge. Clarke left for London in 1845, and his stepson John Chapman ran the pub and the ground. After the First World War the Musters family, who owned both, sold them to the cricket club.

Harold Larwood was a cricketer for Nottinghamshire County Cricket Club and the England cricket team between 1924 and 1938. A right-arm fast bowler who combined extreme speeds with great accuracy, he was considered by many players and commentators to be the finest and the fastest fast bowler of his generation and one of the fastest bowlers of all time.

Bodyline, 1932–33

Bodyline, fast bowling aimed at the batsman's body, was England's tactic in the Ashes in Australia in 1932–33. Its bowlers were Nottinghamshire's Harold Larwood and Bill Voce, whom Douglas Jardine met with their county captain, Arthur Carr, to plan it. The uproar ended Larwood's Test career and changed the laws of cricket; Trent Bridge opened the Larwood and Voce Stand in 1985.

Wikipedia: Bodyline

The oldest professional club

1862

Notts County, called the oldest professional football club in the world, gives 1862 as its foundation, though it was formally organised on 7 December 1864. Its first recorded matches were at the Meadows Cricket Ground, its home until October 1877. It played at Trent Bridge from 1883, and since 1910 at Meadow Lane, on land leased from Nottingham Corporation. In 1894 it beat Bolton Wanderers 4–1 at Goodison Park, the first Second Division team to win the FA Cup. Late in 1901 a set of Notts-style kits sent to Turin gave Juventus their black and white stripes.

James Iremonger

Wilford village

James Iremonger was an English cricketer and one of the players most unlucky never to play Test cricket. He did play a number of minor matches on the 1911–12 Ashes tour and was considered many times between 1901 and 1905 for a place against Australia.

Wikipedia: James Iremonger

James Iremonger was born in Norton, in the East Riding of Yorkshire, on 5 March 1876, and played for Wilford's football club in 1893. He played for Nottingham Forest from 1895 to 1910, won two England caps in 1901–02, played cricket for Nottinghamshire from 1899 to 1914 and coached the county from 1921 to 1938. His brother was Albert Iremonger, the Notts County goalkeeper. He died in Nottingham on 25 March 1956.

Viv Anderson

1978 · Clifton estate

Vivian Alexander Anderson is an English former professional footballer who played as a right-back. He is best known for his ten-year stint with Nottingham Forest, with whom he won the First Division title in 1978, as well as the European Cup in 1979 and 1980. In 1978, he became the first black footballer to play for England's senior men's national team, and from 1980 to 1988, was selected in the England squad for four major tournaments.

Wikipedia: Viv Anderson

Viv Anderson was born in Clifton on 29 July 1956. In ten years at Nottingham Forest, from 1974 to 1984, he played 328 times and won the European Cup twice. In November 1978, against Czechoslovakia, he became the first black footballer to play for England's senior men's team.

William Thompson

Bendigo's tomb, Plaque to William Thompson

William Abednego Thompson, also known as Bendigo Thompson and mononymously as Bendigo, was an English bare-knuckle boxer who won the heavyweight championship of England from James Burke on 12 February 1839. He was inducted into The Ring magazine Hall of Fame in 1955, the International Boxing Hall of Fame in 1991 and the Bare Knuckle Boxing Hall of Fame in 2011. His nickname of "Bendigo" lives on in the name of a city and creek in Australia.

Wikipedia: William Thompson (boxer)

William "Bendigo" Thompson, born in Nottingham on 11 October 1811, won the heavyweight championship of England in 1839. He died in 1880 and was buried in St Mary's burial ground, under a tomb with a crouching lion. The city of Bendigo in Australia is indirectly named after him.

Torvill and Dean

National Ice Centre, Clifton estate

Torvill and Dean are British ice dancers and former British, European, Olympic, and World champions. At the Sarajevo 1984 Winter Olympics the pair won gold and became the highest-scoring figure skaters of all time for a single programme, receiving twelve perfect 6.0s and six 5.9s which included artistic impression scores of 6.0 from every judge, after skating to Maurice Ravel's Boléro.

Wikipedia: Torvill and Dean

The ice dancers Jayne Torvill and Christopher Dean come from Nottingham. The square outside the ice centre is named Bolero Square after their Olympic routine. Jayne Torvill was born in Clifton on 7 October 1957 and worked as an insurance clerk at Norwich Union before skating full-time; at Sarajevo in 1984 she and Christopher Dean won the gold with their free dance to Ravel's Boléro, which earned twelve perfect 6.0 marks.
