Basford

Basford, recorded in Domesday Book, lay along the River Leen. In the 19th century the river's clean water drew bleachers, and the village became an industrial suburb with breweries, a sauce factory and a soap works. It was also home to the poet Philip James Bailey and the motorcycle maker George Brough.

Baseford

1086

Domesday Book, in 1086, calls the village Baseford; the name holds that of an Anglo-Saxon called Basa. It became part of Nottingham in 1877.

Festus at Old Basford

1836

In 1836 the young Nottingham poet Philip James Bailey went to live at his father's house in Old Basford to write. The result was Festus, a vast poem on the Faust story published in 1839, which Tennyson admired.

The bleachers of Basford

1851

The Leen's clean water brought the bleachers. A directory of 1853 counted eight bleaching works in Basford, with many stocking frames and bobbin-net machines, and a population that had grown from 2,124 to 10,093 by 1851.

Shipstones' Star Brewery

1852

James Shipstone founded the Star Brewery in New Basford in 1852. The red-brick building of 1900 had a red illuminated star on its tower that could be seen for miles. Greenall's of Warrington bought the firm in 1978 and stopped brewing in early 1991; the building now belongs to an auction house.

St Leodegarius, Basford

1859

The tower of St Leodegarius fell in 1859; Thomas Allom designed the new one, and the church reopened on 1 March 1860. A fire on 29 November 1900 closed it again until 24 April 1902. It is listed Grade II*.

George Brough

1890 · Brough Superior works, A building and 1 more

George Brough was a motorcycle racer and a world record–holding motorcycle and automobile manufacturer and showman. He was known for his powerful and expensive Brough Superior motorcycles which were the first superbikes. George died in 1970 but his legacy lives on in the many Brough Superior motorcycles maintained by enthusiasts to this day.

HP Sauce and soap

1895

Basford made things for the kitchen and the bathroom. The grocer F. G. Garton started a pickle and sauce factory near his home on Sandon Street and registered the name HP Sauce in 1895. Gerard Brothers opened a soap works in the 1890s; Cussons took it over in 1955, and it closed in 2005.
