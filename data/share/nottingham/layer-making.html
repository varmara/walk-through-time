Made in Nottingham

Nottingham became a city in 1897, as part of Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee. By then it made far more than lace, and three of its firms, Boots, Raleigh and Player's, became household names; by the end of the 20th century Raleigh and Player's had moved the bulk of their making elsewhere, while Boots thrived.

Mapperley bricks

1860s

Boots, 1849–1961

Boots is now one of the largest retailers in the UK and Ireland, by its sales and by its number of shops.

Jesse Boot, 1st Baron Trent transformed The Boots Company, founded by his father, John Boot, into a national retailer, which branded itself as "Chemists to the Nation".

Jesse Boot, born in 1850, turned the business founded by his father, John Boot, into a national retailer. He was knighted in 1909 and became Baron Trent in 1929. He gave the land at Highfields for the new University College, now the University of Nottingham, which opened in 1928; his bust stands there. Boots on Goose Gate

1849

John Boot opened a herbalist's shop on Goose Gate in 1849, and his son Jesse was born in the area, into poverty, the next year. After John died in 1860 his widow Mary kept the shop going as M & J Boot. Jesse became a partner at 21, and in 1883 he built a larger store on the same site, the first of the Boots shops; Nottingham Civic Society put up a plaque here in 2015.

Florence Boot

1886 · Florence Boot Hall

Florence Anne Boot, Lady Trent was a Jersey businesswoman and philanthropist. She assisted her husband, Jesse Boot, in running Boots chemists after their marriage in 1886. Florence was responsible for diversifying the firm's retail offering to include perfume, cosmetics, stationery, books, and other general merchandise and also pioneered in-store cafés.

Wikipedia: Florence Boot

Florence Boot, born in St Helier, Jersey, in 1863, married Jesse Boot in 1886 and helped him run the firm. She brought books and stationery into the Goose Gate shop and started Boots Book-Lovers' Library and cafés in the stores. She founded the university's first hall for women, named Florence Boot Hall after her.

Wikipedia: Florence Boot

Louisa May Long (1892–1981), who nursed with the 94 Nottingham VAD at Radcliffe on Trent in the First World War, was Matron of Florence Boot Hall from 1928 to 1946.

Boots on Pelham Street

1902–05

Boots' central store was built on the corner of Pelham Street and High Street to designs by Albert Nelson Bromley: 1902–05 by Historic England's dates, 1903–04 in Wikipedia's list of his works. Its shopfront, with curving Art Nouveau panels, has hardly changed and is a rare survivor of its kind. Historic England suggests it may be the first Art Nouveau shopfront in the country, and that Harrods inspired it. Boots left in 1972; Zara trades here now.

The Nottingham Blitz, 1941

built in 1377

On the night of 8–9 May 1941 the Luftwaffe bombed Nottingham. Firefighters saved St Mary's Church after an incendiary burned through its roof; three Boots factories, in Poplar Street, Island Street and Station Street, were on fire; University College and the Shakespeare Street chapel were hit. The worst loss was at the Co-op bakery on Meadow Lane, where 49 people died. In all, 159 people were killed that night.

Penicillin at Daleside Road, 1944

1944

In the Second World War the Ministry of Supply asked Boots to design and run a plant that would make penicillin in quantity, by growing the mould on the surface of its broth. Boots built it on Daleside Road, and made huge amounts of penicillin there during the war. It was the largest surface-culture plant in the country, or by another account in the world, and one of the first penicillin factories built anywhere. Its monthly production reports run from August 1944. In 1945 a newly qualified pharmacist, Stewart Adams, later the leader of the team that found ibuprofen, took his first job there.

Boots' research houses, West Bridgford

1920

In 1920 Boots bought a site on Oakfields Road for pharmacological and bacteriological research, which was done here until new laboratories were built in 1956; the site was sold in 1964. With its city-centre labs damaged in the war, Boots put research into converted houses in West Bridgford. From 1953 to 1959 Stewart Adams worked in one of the front rooms of a house here, a rather primitive laboratory, while his technician used the larder as an office. On Rutland Road, in August 1958, his team began to study the phenoxy acids, the line of work that led to ibuprofen. The day nursery on the corner of Oakfields Road and Rutland Road was also part of Boots.

Ibuprofen

Ibuprofen was first made at Boots in Nottingham in December 1961, by Stewart Adams's team. Their later laboratory, on Pennyfoot Street, is now BioCity, and in 2013 a Royal Society of Chemistry blue plaque marked the discovery. Adams and the chemist John Nicholson filed the patent in 1961; the drug was first sold in Britain in 1969, as Brufen, and in 1983 it became the first such painkiller apart from aspirin sold over the counter in the UK.

As the town grew, its pubs, kitchens and wash-houses were supplied from works on its edge.

Shipstones' Star Brewery

1852

James Shipstone founded the Star Brewery in New Basford in 1852. The red-brick building of 1900 had a red illuminated star on its tower that could be seen for miles. Greenall's of Warrington bought the firm in 1978 and stopped brewing in early 1991; the building now belongs to an auction house.

John Robinson founded the Home Brewery in 1875; its beer mats carried a Robin Hood badge. The family sold it in 1986, and its closure cost some 400 jobs. Its building of 1936 is now Sir John Robinson House, and listed Grade II.

George Brough was a motorcycle racer and a world record–holding motorcycle and automobile manufacturer and showman. Rolls-Royce at Hucknall

1927

The airfield opened in 1916, and from 1927 Rolls-Royce used it to test its engines. In the Second World War the first P-51 Mustang fitted with a Rolls-Royce Merlin flew from here, and in December 1940 a German prisoner, Franz von Werra, tried to escape by posing as a Dutch pilot and taking a Hurricane. In the early 1950s the works built the "Flying Bedstead", a test rig for vertical take-off, whose first free flight was on 3 August 1954. The flight test centre closed in 1971.

Tobacco, 1877–2016

John Player bought a tobacco business in 1877. In 1901 it merged with twelve other firms into the Imperial Tobacco Company, and by the late 1950s Player's employed 11,000 people.

John Player

In 1901 the company merged with twelve other companies to become a branch of the Imperial Tobacco Company of Great Britain and Ireland. The company pioneered the advertising with trading cards.
