St Ann's Well

1301

The district takes its name from a spring once thought to heal. It was recorded in 1301 as the Brodewell, and was also called Robin Hood's Well; in 1500 monks built a chapel beside it and dedicated it to St Ann. A spired cover put over it in 1856 was pulled down in 1887 for the railway, and the site was later built over by the Gardeners pub.

Hunger Hills

1551

Edward VI granted the land on Hunger Hill to the Corporation of Nottingham in 1551. In 1604–05 it was enclosed and let in plots to 30 burgesses and their widows, an arrangement that lasted until 1842.

Morley's pottery

c. 1750

The building of St Ann's may have begun in about 1750, when Charles Morley, who had been sheriff, made brown earthenware in a small factory on Beck Street; his brown beer jugs sold well. The pin is approximate.

St Ann's Allotments

1830s

In the 1830s the Clay Fields were divided into plots, and the gardens on Hunger Hill became the St Ann's Allotments, the oldest and largest allotment site in England. Hungerhill Gardens alone has 456 plots over about 52 acres, with sheds and summerhouses from the 19th century; the whole site is listed Grade II*.

New Town

1845

Until the Inclosure Act of 1845 the area was open Clay Fields. The Act let the town take 1,068 acres for housing, and by 1880 the streets of terraced houses called New Town were complete. The terraces north of Victoria Park are listed, as a once common kind of house now mostly lost to bombs and clearance.

St Ann's Church

1863

The church of St Ann was begun for the new district in the 1860s: Sydney Pierrepont, 3rd Earl Manvers, laid its foundation stone on 23 September 1863, and it was consecrated on 26 September 1864, a daughter church of St Mary's. The parish church today is St Ann with Emmanuel. Whether the 1864 church stood here is not known.

The St Ann's riots, 1958

On 23 August 1958 a fight in St Ann's grew into a riot between white and West Indian residents; the police restored order within 90 minutes. A crowd of about 4,000 gathered the following weekend. With the Notting Hill riots a week later, the disturbances led to talks between Caribbean governments and the British government. The pin is approximate.

St Ann's rebuilt

1970

Clearance of the old houses began in 1930, but the war held it up and New Town lasted until 1970, when its Victorian streets were replaced by an estate on the Radburn plan. The film-maker Shane Meadows shot scenes of This Is England here in 2006.
