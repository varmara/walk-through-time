Holme Pierrepont Hall

built about 1500

Built around 1500, one of the few brick houses of its time in Nottinghamshire. The Pierreponts held Holme from about 1280 and became Earls and Dukes of Kingston, then Earls Manvers. It is listed Grade I.

For 700 years the Clifton family owned the manor of Clifton, and Clifton Hall was their manor house.

Won at cards

The Musters family gained the West Bridgford estate in 1523, reputedly by winning it in a game of cards. When they sold land for building they set strict rules for it, and the village grew into a suburb, with its own council from 1894 and part of the borough of Rushcliffe from 1972.

Wikipedia: West Bridgford

Thrumpton Hall

1607–17

The Powdrells, a Roman Catholic family, lost their house here after the Babington Plot; part of it survives inside the hall, with a priest hole. The Pigots' rose-brick house around it was largely complete by 1617.

Wikipedia: Thrumpton Hall

The hall's Jacobean cantilevered staircase was carved from the estate's own timber, under John Webb, a pupil of Inigo Jones.

Wikipedia: Thrumpton Hall

The hall came to the Byrons when George Byron, 8th Baron Byron, married Lucy Wescomb in 1843, and they kept it for about a hundred years. Frederick, the 10th Baron, was the village's vicar and from 1917 its squire as well: its "squarson".

Wikipedia: Thrumpton Hall

George Fitzroy Seymour owned the estate from 1949 to 1994, and Miranda Seymour has owned it since.

Wikipedia: Thrumpton Hall

The storming of Shelford House

In November 1645 Parliament's troops under Sydnam Poyntz, with Colonel John Hutchinson leading the assault, stormed Shelford House, held for the King by Philip Stanhope. About 160 of its defenders were killed, and the house was burnt that night.

Wikipedia: Storming of Shelford House
