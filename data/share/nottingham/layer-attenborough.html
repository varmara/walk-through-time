Attenborough

Attenborough is an old village on the Trent, known in Saxon times as Addensburgh. The Civil War general Henry Ireton came from here, and its churchyard holds the memorial to those killed in the Chilwell explosion of 1918. Gravel digging in the 20th century left the lakes of the nature reserve.

Addensburgh

In Saxon times the village was known as Addensburgh, and a ferry crossed the Trent here from before 1774. In 1966 a hoard of Roman coins was found on a footpath near the railway and Barrett Lane.

Henry Ireton

1611

Henry Ireton, eldest son of German Ireton of Attenborough, was baptised in St Mary's Church here on 3 November 1611. He fought for Parliament at Edgehill in October 1642. On the night before Naseby, in June 1645, he surprised the Royalist army; the next day he was wounded and captured, and Cromwell later freed him. He married Bridget, Cromwell's eldest daughter, was one of the commissioners who signed the death warrant of Charles I, and died of disease outside Limerick on 26 November 1651.

The Chilwell explosion memorial

1918

In St Mary's churchyard a memorial remembers the 134 people killed on 1 July 1918 (137 by another count) in the explosion at the shell factory in Chilwell, still the largest number of deaths caused by a single explosion in mainland Britain.

Gravel pits and floods

2000

Attenborough Nature Reserve is a series of gravel pits, flooded once the gravel had been dug out. The village itself flooded in November 2000, and its flood defences were finished in the summer of 2012.
