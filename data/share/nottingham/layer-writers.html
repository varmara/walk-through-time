Writers and artists

Nottinghamshire has produced or attracted a number of notable writers and artists. Their work often draws on the life of the area, from the Byrons and the Romantic poets to the mining communities of D. H. Lawrence and the working-class Nottingham of Alan Sillitoe.

Writers

Writers connected with Nottingham include Lucy Hutchinson in the 17th century, Byron and Henry Kirke White in the Romantic period, and in the 20th century D. H. Lawrence, born in Eastwood, and Alan Sillitoe, whose novel Saturday Night and Sunday Morning is set in the city.

The Wise Men of Gotham

15th century

The "fools of Gotham" were already mentioned in the fifteenth century, in the Towneley plays. A legend says that the villagers played the fool, even trying to fence in a cuckoo, so that King John would build his road and hunting lodge elsewhere. In 1807 Washington Irving called New York "Gotham", and Batman's Gotham City took the name.

Lucy Hutchinson

Owthorpe

Lucy Hutchinson was an English translator, poet, and biographer, and the first person to translate the complete text of Lucretius's De rerum natura into English verse, during the years of the Interregnum.

Wikipedia: Lucy Hutchinson

Lucy Hutchinson, born in the Tower of London in 1620, was the first to translate the whole of Lucretius into English verse, and her Order and Disorder is perhaps the first epic poem by a woman in English. Her life of her husband, John Hutchinson, written for her family, was printed in 1806. She died at Owthorpe in 1681.

Wikipedia: Lucy Hutchinson

Clifton Grove

1677

A local account says Sir William Clifton planted the avenue of elms in 1677, from a little stone bridge over Fairham Brook up to the hall; it was said to be wide enough for six carriages abreast. In 1853 it was "an avenue of trees a mile in length". By the 18th century the Grove was a favourite outing, and an 1874 guide tells of thousands of Nottingham artisans there at Easter and Whitsuntide. The Nottingham poet Henry Kirke White, a butcher's son, published Clifton Grove, a Sketch in Verse, in 1803, aged 17. D. H. Lawrence set a scene of Sons and Lovers (1913) in the Grove.

Wikipedia: Henry Kirke White

The legend of Clifton Grove

A ballad, Bateman's Tragedy, tells of a young man, Bateman, and a beautiful heiress of Clifton who pledge to marry; she breaks her word for a richer widower, and on her wedding day he hangs himself at her door. His ghost haunts her, and in the end carries her off. In the version told locally, the jilted lover drowns himself in the Trent and the bride is dragged into the river at the chasm in the Grove; in another, a Clifton beauty is murdered and thrown down the cliff.

Mary Howitt

Howitt House, South Parade

Mary Howitt was an English writer, editor, translator and a pioneer of the women's rights movement in the UK. She is most known as the author of the famous poem The Spider and the Fly. Howitt translated several works by Hans Christian Andersen and Frederika Bremer.

Wikipedia: Mary Howitt

Mary Howitt married William Howitt in 1821, and by 1823 they were living in Nottingham, on Timber Hill, now South Parade, next to Smith's Bank, for over twelve years. She wrote The Spider and the Fly and translated Hans Christian Andersen's tales.

The Byron vault

1824 · 12th century

St Mary Magdalene's stands on the site of a Saxon church. Its tower was built in stages between the 12th and 14th centuries, and its porch in 1320. Most of the Lords Byron lie in the family vault here, among them the poet Lord Byron, and his daughter Ada, Countess of Lovelace, rests there too. Byron died in Greece on 19 April 1824; Westminster Abbey refused to bury him, and after he lay in state in London he was brought here. The church is listed Grade II*.

Wikipedia: Church of St Mary Magdalene, Hucknall

Saturday Night and Sunday Morning

1958

Alan Sillitoe's 1958 novel became a 1960 film directed by Karel Reisz, with Albert Finney as Arthur Seaton, who works at the Raleigh factory. Much of the exterior location filming took place in Nottingham, and shooting at a Nottingham factory ran two days over schedule and £3,500 over budget.

Nottingham Contemporary

2009

Garners Hill, said to be the oldest site in Nottingham, once held cave dwellings, a Saxon fort and a medieval town hall, until the Victorians cleared it for a railway line. On 14 November 2009 an art centre designed by Caruso St John opened here, its walls embossed with a Nottingham lace pattern.
