The Hemlock Stone

The Hemlock Stone is a pillar of red Triassic sandstone, more than 200 million years old, on the hill above Stapleford. A hard cap of barytes protected its top while water, ice and wind wore away the softer rock beneath. Stories have grown round it: Beltane fires lit on it into the 19th century, witches and hemlock, a giant who threw it, and the Devil, who flung it at Lenton Priory, where it fell short.

Stapleford Cross

c. 1000

The Anglo-Saxon cross in St Helen's churchyard dates from around AD 1000, though some put it as early as the 8th century. Its shaft is carved with interlace and a winged figure with a spear, perhaps an archangel or St Luke. Its head was lost in the 18th century; after several moves it came to the churchyard in 1928. Pevsner called it by far the most important pre-Conquest monument in Nottinghamshire, and it is listed Grade I.

St Helen's, Stapleford

15th century

St Helen's was given its clerestory towards the end of the 15th century. Its monuments include Robert Tevery, who died in 1571, and Gervase Tevery, who died in 1639. A classical porch was added in 1785, the church was restored in 1875–77, and a memorial chapel came in 1923. It is listed Grade II*.
