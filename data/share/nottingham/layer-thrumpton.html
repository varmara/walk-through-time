Thrumpton

Thrumpton is a small estate village on the Trent. Its hall was built by the Pigot family in the early 17th century, and later owners built much of the village. The estate has passed through several families, including the Byrons.

All Saints Church

13th century

A church was built here in the 13th century. George Edmund Street restored it in 1871, and it is listed Grade II*.

Thrumpton Hall

1617

The Powdrells, a Roman Catholic family, had a house here, which they lost after the Babington Plot. Around it the Pigots built a rose-brick house, largely complete by 1617. Part of the Powdrells' house survives inside it, with a priest hole.

The staircase

1617

The Pigot family built Thrumpton Hall in rose-coloured brick, and it was largely complete by 1617. In the 1660s Gervase Pigot altered and improved it. Its Jacobean cantilevered staircase was carved from the estate's own timber, with John Webb, a pupil of Inigo Jones, overseeing the work.

The Byrons of Thrumpton

1607–17

The hall came to the Byrons when George Byron, 8th Baron Byron, married Lucy Wescomb in 1843, and they kept it for about a hundred years. Frederick, the 10th Baron, was the village's vicar and from 1917 its squire as well: its "squarson".
