Gylden Broc

Giltbrook's name is thought to come from the Old English Gylden Broc, the golden brook, after the stream that runs down from the fields north of where IKEA now stands. A retail park was built beside IKEA in 2008.

Forty Bridges

1873

Between May 1873 and November 1875 Joseph Firbank built a viaduct here for the Great Northern Railway, 43 arches and 1,716 feet long, known locally as Forty Bridges. It was pulled down in 1973 to make way for the A610 bypass.

The Pentrich rising, 1817

On the night of 9–10 June 1817, men from around Pentrich in Derbyshire set out from South Wingfield to march on Nottingham, led by Jeremiah Brandreth, the Nottingham Captain. At Giltbrook they met a small force of Light Dragoons and scattered. The news that had set them marching was a hoax by William Oliver, a government informer; Brandreth and two others were hanged and beheaded at Derby.
