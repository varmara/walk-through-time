Ruddington

The lost church of Flawford

St Peter's, the old church of Flawford, closed in 1718 and was pulled down in 1779. Three alabaster figures found in it, probably hidden around 1539, are now at Nottingham Castle Museum, and Ruddington's chapel took its name.

Framework Knitters' Museum

c. 1840

Ruddington's framework knitters worked at hand frames in workshops behind their cottages. Workshops of about 1840 and workers' cottages of about 1829, all listed, now make up the Framework Knitters' Museum.

Ruddington Depot

Built from 1940 to fill and store ammunition, the depot was closed in 1983. Its land is now a transport heritage centre, a business park and Rushcliffe Country Park.

Holme Pierrepont

Holme Pierrepont Hall

built about 1500

Built around 1500, one of the few brick houses of its time in Nottinghamshire. The Pierreponts held Holme from about 1280 and became Earls and Dukes of Kingston, then Earls Manvers. It is listed Grade I.

Wikipedia: Holme Pierrepont Hall

National Watersports Centre

Dug out of a former gravel works, the centre opened in 1971, and hosted the World Rowing Championships in 1975 and 1986. Its whitewater course was built in 1986.

The Civil War

Colonel Hutchinson

John Hutchinson, born at Owthorpe in 1615, held Nottingham Castle for Parliament from 1643 and signed Charles I's death warrant. He died a prisoner in 1664 and is buried at Owthorpe; his wife Lucy's Memoirs of his life made him famous.

Wikipedia: John Hutchinson (Roundhead)

The storming of Shelford House

In November 1645 Parliament's troops under Sydnam Poyntz, with Colonel John Hutchinson leading the assault, stormed Shelford House, held for the King by Philip Stanhope. About 160 of its defenders were killed, and the house was burnt that night.

Wikipedia: Storming of Shelford House

Gotham

The Wise Men of Gotham

A legend says that the villagers played the fool, even trying to fence in a cuckoo, so that King John would build his road and hunting lodge elsewhere. In 1807 Washington Irving called New York "Gotham", and Batman's Gotham City took the name.

Wikipedia: Wise Men of Gotham

Tollerton

Tollerton airfield

1941

Licensed in 1929 and opened in 1930 by Sir Sefton Brancker, it was one of Britain's first municipal aerodromes. In the Second World War it repaired Lancaster bombers; its 18 concrete pillboxes are all listed.

Wikipedia: Nottingham (Tollerton) Airport

Coal and power

Cotgrave Colliery

The colliery came in the early 1960s: from 1962 hundreds of mining families moved here from Radford's closed pit, and the village grew several times over. The pit worked through the 1984–85 strike and closed in 1993.

Wikipedia: Cotgrave

Ratcliffe-on-Soar Power Station

Generating from 1968, with eight cooling towers, it was the last coal-fired power station in Britain. When it shut in September 2024 it ended 142 years of coal power in the country.

Wikipedia: Ratcliffe-on-Soar Power Station
