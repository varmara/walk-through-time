Bestwood

Bestwood began as a royal hunting park, recorded from 1286. In the 19th century it belonged to the Dukes of St Albans, who built a new lodge, and in the 1870s and 1880s its colliery and ironworks brought industry and a new village to the old parkland.

A royal hunting park

1286

The first written record of a royal hunting lodge at Bestwood is from 1286, in the reign of Edward I. Charles II and Nell Gwyn visited it, and in 1682 he leased the lodge to her. Their son, Charles Beauclerk, Earl of Burford, was made Duke of St Albans in 1684, and Bestwood passed down through his descendants.

Bestwood Lodge

1862

The present lodge was built in 1862–65 for the 10th Duke of St Albans by Samuel Sanders Teulon, in the Gothic Revival style. The army took it over in the Second World War, and in the mid-1970s it became a hotel. It is listed Grade II*.

Bestwood winding engine

1873

The Bestwood Coal and Iron Company's colliery here wound up its coal and lowered its miners with a vertical steam engine of 1873. The pit closed in 1967, but the winding engine house and its engine survive.

Coal and iron at Bestwood

1875

The Lancaster family sank Bestwood's coal mine in 1875. Beside it the Bestwood Coal and Iron Company opened an ironworks in 1881 with two blast furnaces, added two more in 1890, and kept it working until 1928. The company built the terraces of Park Road, St Albans Road and The Square for its workers, and its offices, with their clock tower, on Park Road.
