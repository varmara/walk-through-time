Wilford

A village on the south bank of the Trent, reached for centuries by ferry and, since the 1870s, by bridge.

The river and its bridges

Ferry Inn

Late 17th century

Wilford stayed a village until the later 19th century. With woods around it and riverside amenities such as the Ferry Inn, it attracted many visitors from Nottingham. The Notts County goalkeeper Albert Iremonger was its landlord until his death in 1958.

Wilford Toll Bridge

built in 1870

Opened in 1870, replacing a ferry that had crossed here for some 400 years. Pedestrians paid a halfpenny toll. It was closed to traffic in 1974.

Wikipedia: Wilford Toll Bridge

Coal was found on the Clifton estate, and work on Clifton Colliery began in 1868. Sir Robert Juckes Clifton, the last of the Clifton baronets, died of typhoid in 1869; the colliery and this toll bridge, both his projects, opened in 1870.

Wikipedia: Robert Juckes Clifton

Wilford Suspension Bridge

built in 1907

A footbridge built by the city's water department, mainly to carry water pipes across the Trent to the reservoir on Wilford Hill. The engineer was Arthur Brown.

The village

St Wilfrid's Church

Late 14th century

The church dates from the late 14th century and is thought to have been founded by Gervase de Wilford in around 1361. Inside are two memorials to the poet Henry Kirke White, who drew much inspiration from Wilford and Clifton.

Wikipedia: St Wilfrid's Church, Wilford

Henry Kirke White is said to have spent much of his time writing poems in the churchyard, sitting under his favourite tree.

Wilford House

1781

Thomas Forman, who founded the Nottingham Post, bought Wilford House from the Smith family after the newspaper's successful launch.

The Nottingham Captain

Jeremiah Brandreth, born in Wilford, led the Pentrich rising of 1817 and was known as the Nottingham Captain. He was hanged for high treason outside Derby Gaol that November, and then beheaded.

Wikipedia: Jeremiah Brandreth

Wilford Power Station

A coal-fired power station on the north bank of the Trent, opened in 1925. It closed in 1981 and was soon demolished; the Riverside Retail Park stands on its site.
