The manor of Wilford

1280

Before the Conquest the manor of Wilford belonged to Gode the Countess. In Domesday Book the settlement is named Willesforde, with a fishery, a priest and 23 sokemen. Clifton and Wilford, Peverel lands, passed to the Crown early in Henry II's reign, and the king granted them to Gerbode de Escalt; in Richard I's time they passed to the de Rodes family. Before 1280 Gervase de Clifton obtained both manors from Sir Gerard de Rodes for £30 a year, and a charter of 1280 confirmed the grant. In 1853 Sir J. G. J. Clifton was lord of the manor, owned all its land but the church's glebe, and was patron of the church; in 1870–72 the manor still belonged to Sir R. J. Clifton.

St Wilfrid's Church

c. 1361

Around 1361 Gervase de Wilford is thought to have founded a church here, and the building dates from the late 14th century; it was restored in 1890–92 by Naylor and Sale. Inside are two memorials to the poet Henry Kirke White, who drew much inspiration from Wilford and Clifton.

The village and its big house

The village of the 17th to the early 19th century: its inn, Wilford House, a sea captain, a poet and the Nottingham Captain.

Ferry Inn

17th century

The inn was made from two houses and an outbuilding of the late 17th century, and is listed Grade II. Wilford stayed a village until the later 19th century. With woods around it and riverside amenities such as the Ferry Inn, it attracted many visitors from Nottingham. The Notts County goalkeeper Albert Iremonger was its landlord until his death in 1958.

Captain John Deane

1710

In 1710 Captain John Deane's ship, the Nottingham Galley, sank off Boon Island, New England. He later served in the Russian navy under Peter the Great, and in 1736 he retired to Wilford. He is buried in St Wilfrid's churchyard.

Wilford House

1781

The house was designed in 1781 for the Smith family as Wilford Hall, the centre of an estate with stables, lodges for the house staff and a private chapel. It is a red-brick country house of three storeys and five bays, listed Grade II, and its stable block of the same year is listed too. The Smiths' bank in Nottingham is believed to have been the first bank outside London, and Samuel Smith, an MP for 44 years, is believed to have built the house; by 1790 it was his. The banker Abel Smith died at Wilford on 12 July 1788. His wife's younger sister, Elizabeth Bird, was the mother of William Wilberforce, the slavery abolitionist, who stayed at the house for several months between 1786 and 1789. In 1853 it was the seat of Henry Smith, a large, handsome brick mansion; he owned about 70 acres of the parish, and in 1828 he built an infant school in the village. Thomas Forman, who founded the Nottingham Post, later bought the house from the Smiths, and it passed to the Forman Hardy family; at some point it was renamed Wilford House. It became offices in the 1980s, the Wilford Place estate was built on its grounds in 2006, and in 2021 the house was given a multi-million pound refurbishment.

The Nottingham Captain

Jeremiah Brandreth, born in Wilford, led the Pentrich rising of 1817 and was known as the Nottingham Captain. He was hanged for high treason outside Derby Gaol that November, and then beheaded.

Bridges, coal and the suburb

From 1868 a colliery, toll and suspension bridges and a power station, as the village grew into a suburb.

Sir Robert Clifton's colliery

Coal was found on the Clifton estate, and work on Clifton Colliery began in 1868. Sir Robert Juckes Clifton, the last of the Clifton baronets, died of typhoid in 1869; the colliery and this toll bridge, both his projects, opened in 1870.

Wilford Toll Bridge

1870

For some 400 years a ferry crossed the Trent here, until a temporary wooden footbridge replaced it in 1864. On 16 June 1870 the toll bridge opened, and pedestrians paid a halfpenny to cross; it is still known locally as the Halfpenny Bridge. It was closed to traffic in 1974, and in 1980 its centre span was replaced by a narrower footbridge. In 2014–15 it was widened again to carry the trams of Nottingham Express Transit, and it is now a tram, pedestrian and cycle bridge.

Wikipedia: Wilford Toll Bridge

The old Manor House

A house called the Manor House stood here, near the river at the south end of the village green, and is said to have once been a home of the Clifton family. It appears on an 1885 map. Manor House Close was built on its site; next to it stands the only house left of the row once called Manor House and Main Road Cottages.

Village to suburb

1891

Wilford parish had 2,769 people in 1891 and about 4,500 by 1901. South Wilford became part of West Bridgford in 1935, and in 1952 Wilford became part of the City of Nottingham. The Silverdale estate was built on its farmland in the late 1950s, and Compton Acres in 1986.

Wilford Suspension Bridge

1906

Opened in 1906 to carry water mains across the Trent to the reservoir on Wilford Hill, with a footway over them; it was designed by Arthur Brown of Elliott & Brown. It was restored in 1983 and again in 2008–2010.

Sportsmen of Wilford

Three footballers born or living in Wilford, two of them Iremongers.

Harry Cursham

Wilford village

Henry Alfred Cursham was an English footballer and cricketer. He played football mostly for Notts County, with spells at Corinthian, Grantham Town and Thursday Wanderers. In cricket, he played two first class games for Nottinghamshire.

James Iremonger

Wilford village

James Iremonger was an English cricketer and one of the players most unlucky never to play Test cricket. He did play a number of minor matches on the 1911–12 Ashes tour and was considered many times between 1901 and 1905 for a place against Australia.

The churchyard holds the war graves of eight soldiers of the First World War and an airman of the Second. Albert Harvey Iremonger, killed in action on 14 July 1943, aged 27, is buried here.
