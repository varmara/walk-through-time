The map of Nottingham

Stories of Nottingham

  • Old inns 3 places
    Three pubs in the centre each claim to be the oldest in Nottingham. A television investigation concluded that the Salutation is the oldest building, but the Bell has been a pub the longest.
  • Churches 5 places
    Medieval Nottingham had three parishes: St Mary's, St Peter's and St Nicholas'. Each still has its church, joined in the 1840s by a Catholic cathedral, and by chapels of the Nonconformists, such as the Unitarians on High Pavement.
  • Lace and trade 3 places
    In the 19th century Nottingham was a world centre of lace. Merchants built warehouses and showrooms in the Lace Market, east of the Old Market Square.
  • Civic Nottingham 7 places
    The buildings where the town and county were governed, judged and connected to the railway.
  • Theatres and culture 10 places
    Theatres, concert halls, a library and galleries, from a Georgian subscription library to a 21st-century art centre.
  • Wilford 10 places
    A village on the south bank of the Trent, reached for centuries by ferry and, since the 1870s, by bridge.
  • West Bridgford and Trent Bridge 14 places
    South of the river: Test cricket, Nottingham Forest and the county's headquarters.
  • The Meadows and the river 5 places
    The low ground between the city and the Trent, with two football grounds facing each other across the river.
  • Clifton 5 places
    An old village above the Trent, and next to it one of the largest housing estates ever built by a council.
  • Sneinton 4 places
    The hill east of the city: a windmill, cave dwellings, a church by Thomas Rickman and the old wholesale market.
  • Caves 3 places
    Nottingham stands on soft sandstone that can be dug with hand tools, and about a thousand man-made caves are known beneath it: cellars, workshops, dwellings and shelters.
  • People 225 places
    People connected with buildings on the map: who designed them, who lived, worked or played in them, and who is remembered on their walls. Entries come from commemorative plaques (Open Plaques), architects recorded in Wikidata, and hand-written entries checked against Wikipedia and Historic England.
  • Barton in Fabis and Thrumpton 9 places
    Two villages on the south bank of the Trent, upstream from Clifton: Barton, once Barton in the Beans, and Thrumpton, with its Jacobean hall.
  • Rushcliffe 11 places
    The borough south of the Trent: villages of stocking knitters, a Tudor hall and the gravel pits that became a centre for watersports, a Civil War massacre and the regicide who led it, the village of fools, an airfield, a mining village, and the last coal-fired power station in Britain.
  • Science and industry 10 places
    Where Nottingham invented, made and mined: from the stocking frame to the bicycle, the medicines of Boots, the scanner that sees inside the body, and the water, coal and shells the city depended on.
  • The industrial revolution 9 places
    How Nottingham and its coalfield helped make the industrial age: an early railway of wood, the first mill of the factory system, the canal and the railway that carried the coal, the lace machine, the frame-breakers who fought the machines, and the iron and steam that outlasted the pits.
  • Boots 7 places
    Jesse Boot's chemist's shop grew into a national company: its factories, its laboratories and the gifts he left the city.
  • Coal and power 10 places
    The coalfield round Nottingham: the pits, the wagonway, canal and railways that carried the coal, and the power stations by the Trent that burned it.
  • Faith 20 places
    Churches and chapels from the medieval parish churches to the Methodist missions, and the preachers who founded movements here.
  • Great houses and estates 13 places
    The halls of the families who owned the land round Nottingham, and the villages and estates they shaped.
  • Knitting, lace and the machines 12 places
    How the stocking frame and the lace machine made Nottingham's trade: the inventors, the mills and workshops, the merchants' warehouses, and the frame-breakers who fought the machines.
  • Medicine and science 11 places
    Doctors, engineers and scientists: from the miller who was a mathematician to the scanner that looks inside the body.
  • Sport 14 places
    Cricket at Trent Bridge, football on both banks of the Trent, prize-fighters, skaters and rowers.
  • The Trent, bridges and getting about 16 places
    Crossing and taming the river, and the canal, railways, bicycles and aircraft that moved people and goods.
  • Riot, rebellion and war 21 places
    Nottingham in times of conflict: the castle and the Civil War, the risings and riots of the 19th century, and the two World Wars at home.
  • Women's lives and votes for women 19 places
    Nottingham women who campaigned for the vote, ran schools, businesses and libraries, healed, built and wrote.
  • Writers and artists 17 places
    The poets, novelists, painters and photographers who lived, worked or were born here.
