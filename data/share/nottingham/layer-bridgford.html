Trent Bridge

A fort at the bridge

The settlement began in the early 10th century, when defences and houses were built at the south end of Trent Bridge to guard Nottingham against the Danes. Domesday, in 1086, records it as part of the manor of Clifton.

Trent Bridge cricket ground

The first recorded match was played on ground behind the Trent Bridge Inn in 1838, and William Clarke opened the ground in 1841. It hosted its first Test match, England against Australia, in 1899.

City Ground

Nottingham Forest moved here in 1898, to their seventh home. The name dates from 1897, when Nottingham was granted city status, and Notts County's Meadow Lane is only three hundred yards away, on the other side of the Trent.

West Bridgford and Lady Bay

Won at cards

The Musters family gained the West Bridgford estate in 1523, reputedly by winning it in a game of cards. When they sold land for building they set strict rules for it, and the village grew into a suburb, with its own council from 1894 and part of the borough of Rushcliffe from 1972.

Wikipedia: West Bridgford

St Giles' Church

13th century

A medieval church of which little remains. Thomas Chambers Hine restored it in 1872, and in 1896 a new nave and chancel were begun, their foundation stone laid by Lady Byron of Thrumpton Hall.

The Lady Bay bombs

In 1941 a German bomber dropped a line of bombs across Lady Bay. New houses went up on the bomb sites in the 1950s and 1960s, and Pinders Ponds, to the east, are thought to be flooded bomb craters.

Wikipedia: Lady Bay, Nottinghamshire

The first out-of-town superstore

In 1964 an American company opened what was called the UK's first major out-of-town superstore on Loughborough Road. In 1966 the young Asda chain took it over, and in 1999 replaced it with a much larger store on the land next to it.

Lady Bay Bridge

The Midland Railway built this bridge for its direct line to Melton Mowbray and London. The line closed in 1969, and in 1979 the bridge was turned over to road traffic.

Edwalton and Compton Acres

Church of the Holy Rood

12th century

A church dating from the 12th century, listed Grade II*. Arthur Brewill and Basil Baily rebuilt its chancel in 1894, and its east window has glass by Morris & Co.

Wikipedia: Church of the Holy Rood, Edwalton

Edwalton station

The station opened in 1880 on the Midland Railway's direct line from Nottingham to Melton Mowbray, which let its London expresses avoid Leicester. It closed to passengers in 1941, and its site is now housing. Beyond it the line became the Old Dalby test track, where the Advanced Passenger Train was tried.

Wikipedia: Edwalton railway station

Compton Acres

A district built mostly in the 1990s, named after a garden in Poole. Its tram, opened in 2015, runs along a disused railway line.
