The Civil War

Charles I

Plaque to Charles I

Charles I was King of England, Scotland, and Ireland from 27 March 1625 until his execution in 1649. Charles was born into the House of Stuart as the second son of King James VI of Scotland. After his father inherited the English throne in 1603, he moved to England, where he spent much of the rest of his life.

Colonel Hutchinson

John Hutchinson, born at Owthorpe in 1615, held Nottingham Castle for Parliament from 1643 and signed Charles I's death warrant. He died a prisoner in 1664 and is buried at Owthorpe; his wife Lucy's Memoirs of his life made him famous.

Wikipedia: John Hutchinson (Roundhead)

The storming of Shelford House

In November 1645 Parliament's troops under Sydnam Poyntz, with Colonel John Hutchinson leading the assault, stormed Shelford House, held for the King by Philip Stanhope. About 160 of its defenders were killed, and the house was burnt that night.

Wikipedia: Storming of Shelford House

Risings and riots

The first Luddites

The Luddite movement began in Arnold on 11 March 1811. Across Nottinghamshire the frame-breakers went after the "wide" knitting frames used to make cheap goods.

Wikipedia: Luddites

The Nottingham Captain

Jeremiah Brandreth, born in Wilford, led the Pentrich rising of 1817 and was known as the Nottingham Captain. He was hanged for high treason outside Derby Gaol that November, and then beheaded.

Wikipedia: Jeremiah Brandreth

The castle burned, 1831

1674–79

When the House of Lords threw out the Second Reform Bill on 8 October 1831, rioting broke out in Nottingham and a crowd burned the castle, which belonged to the Duke of Newcastle, an opponent of reform. Three men were hanged; the Duke refused to restore the castle, and its burnt shell stood on the rock for years.

Wikipedia: 1831 reform riots

Lowe's Silk Mill

During the Reform riots of 1831, Lowe's Silk Mill in Beeston was burnt on 11 October, the day after Nottingham Castle, and the same day the riots ceased.

Albert Ball

Plaque to Albert Ball

Albert Ball, was a British fighter pilot during the First World War. At the time of his death he was the United Kingdom's leading flying ace, with 44 victories, and remained its fourth-highest scorer behind Edward Mannock, James McCudden and George McElroy. Born and raised in Nottingham, Ball joined the Sherwood Foresters at the outbreak of the First World War and was commissioned as a second lieutenant in October 1914.

Wikipedia: Albert Ball

Plaque by Nottingham Civic Society;Robin Hood Rifles: “Onetime home of Albert Ball VC of the Robin Hood Rifles and the Royal Flying Corps. Lost in action over France 7 May 1917.”

National Shell Filling Factory

Viscount Chetwynd built the factory from 1915 to fill shells with explosive; it filled some 19 million during the First World War. On 1 July 1918 eight tons of TNT exploded, killing 134 people. The site is now Chetwynd Barracks.

Wikipedia: National Shell Filling Factory, Chilwell

City War Memorial

1927

Designed by the City Engineer Thomas Wallis Gordon, on land beside the Trent given by Sir Jesse Boot, and unveiled on 11 November 1927. It remembers the men of Nottingham who died in both World Wars.

Wikipedia: City War Memorial, Nottingham

Tollerton airfield

1941

Licensed in 1929 and opened in 1930 by Sir Sefton Brancker, it was one of Britain's first municipal aerodromes. In the Second World War it repaired Lancaster bombers; its 18 concrete pillboxes are all listed.

Wikipedia: Nottingham (Tollerton) Airport

Ruddington Depot

Built from 1940 to fill and store ammunition, the depot was closed in 1983. Its land is now a transport heritage centre, a business park and Rushcliffe Country Park.

The Lady Bay bombs

In 1941 a German bomber dropped a line of bombs across Lady Bay. New houses went up on the bomb sites in the 1950s and 1960s, and Pinders Ponds, to the east, are thought to be flooded bomb craters.

Wikipedia: Lady Bay, Nottinghamshire

Vera Anderson

Capital One

Vera Anderson, a Boots nurse, was on duty when bombs wrecked the Boots Printing Works here on 8 May 1941. She gave first aid to a dying night watchman through burning debris, then treated casualties at the control centre, and was awarded the George Medal.
