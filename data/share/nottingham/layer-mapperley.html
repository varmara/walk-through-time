Mapperley

Mapperley takes its name from Thomas Mapurley, a 14th-century official. The high ground north-east of the town was the estate of the Wright family, and in the 19th century it was chosen for hospitals and asylums. Its clay also supplied some of the area's largest brickworks.

Thomas Mapurley

1387

The name goes back at least to the 14th century. Thomas Mapurley was under-sheriff of Nottinghamshire from about 1387 and mayor of Nottingham in 1402–03. The high ground to the north-east was long known as Mapperley Plains.

Mapperley Hall and the Wrights

1790s

In the 1790s Ichabod Wright built Mapperley Hall at the heart of his estate. His grandson, Colonel Charles Ichabod Wright, began selling land for houses in 1873, but most of the estate was released only in 1903; some 163 houses were approved in Mapperley Park between 1906 and 1914.

Coppice Hospital

1859

T. C. Hine designed the Coppice Hospital, a hospital on the hill off Ransom Road, built in 1857–59. It closed in 1985, and the building is now flats.

The Battle of Mapperley Hills, 1842

1842

In August 1842 a meeting of more than two thousand in the Market Place voted to stop work until the People's Charter became law. The magistrates banned further meetings, and for days troops and police broke up gatherings across the town. On 23 August Chartists from Hucknall, Bulwell, Arnold, Basford and other places met on the common on Mapperley Hills; the 2nd Dragoon Guards were sent, and when the crowd, sitting on the grass to eat, would not disperse, Colonel Rolleston had about four hundred arrested. As they were marched into the town, a crowd stoned the soldiers and police. About fifty went for trial, some getting hard labour. Afterwards the town's workers turned instead against the Corn Laws and the price of bread.

Mapperley bricks

1860s

The clay of the high ground fed some of the area's biggest brickworks. In the 1860s Edward Gripper mechanised them, with Hoffmann kilns, and brickmaking went on until 1969. Mapperley bricks are said to have built St Pancras station, and so, people said, Nottingham once stood on Mapperley Plains.

St Jude's, Mapperley

1877

St Jude's on Woodborough Road opened in 1877 on land given by the Wright family, as a daughter church of St Ann's. Its chancel came in 1893 and its aisles in 1916, and it became a parish of its own on 9 November 1926.

Mapperley Hospital

1880

George Thomas Hine designed the Nottingham Borough Lunatic Asylum, opened in August 1880 with its wards along one long corridor. It was extended in 1889, joined the NHS as Mapperley Hospital in 1948, and closed in December 1994. The north end is now Duncan Macmillan House, the local NHS trust's headquarters, and the south end, Nightingale House, is flats.
