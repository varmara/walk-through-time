Captain John Deane

1710

In 1710 Captain John Deane's ship, the Nottingham Galley, sank off Boon Island, New England. He later served in the Russian navy under Peter the Great, and in 1736 he retired to Wilford. He is buried in St Wilfrid's churchyard.

The Sherwood Foresters

1779

A regiment raised in 1741 became, at the request of the people of Nottinghamshire, the 45th (1st Nottinghamshire) Regiment of Foot in 1779. Against Napoleon it fought through the Peninsular War from 1808 to 1814, and at Talavera in 1809 won the nickname "Old Stubborns". Renamed the Sherwood Foresters in 1866, it was joined with the Derbyshire regiment on 1 July 1881 as the Sherwood Foresters (Nottinghamshire and Derbyshire Regiment), whose museum is in the castle.

Wikipedia: 45th (Nottinghamshire) (Sherwood Foresters) Regiment of Foot

The First World War

A shell filling factory at Chilwell, the explosion of 1918, and how the city remembers those who died in the war.

Louisa May Long

1914 · Florence Boot Hall

Florence Boot, born in St Helier, Jersey, in 1863, married Jesse Boot in 1886 and helped him run the firm. She brought books and stationery into the Goose Gate shop and started Boots Book-Lovers' Library and cafés in the stores. She founded the university's first hall for women, named Florence Boot Hall after her.

Wikipedia: Florence Boot

Louisa May Long (1892–1981), who nursed with the 94 Nottingham VAD at Radcliffe on Trent in the First World War, was Matron of Florence Boot Hall from 1928 to 1946.

Nottingham Women's History Group: List of Notable Nottingham Women

National Shell Filling Factory

Viscount Chetwynd built the factory from 1915 to fill shells with explosive; it filled some 19 million during the First World War. On 1 July 1918 eight tons of TNT exploded, killing 134 people. The site is now Chetwynd Barracks.

Albert Ball

1917 · Plaque to Albert Ball

Albert Ball, was a British fighter pilot during the First World War. At the time of his death he was the United Kingdom's leading flying ace, with 44 victories, and remained its fourth-highest scorer behind Edward Mannock, James McCudden and George McElroy. Born and raised in Nottingham, Ball joined the Sherwood Foresters at the outbreak of the First World War and was commissioned as a second lieutenant in October 1914.

Wikipedia: Albert Ball

The Chilwell explosion memorial

In St Mary's churchyard a memorial remembers the 134 people killed on 1 July 1918 in the explosion at the shell factory in Chilwell, still the largest number of deaths caused by a single explosion in mainland Britain.

The Second World War

Depots and airfields, bombs on Lady Bay and the Blitz of May 1941.

Tollerton airfield

1941

Licensed in 1929 and opened in 1930 by Sir Sefton Brancker, it was one of Britain's first municipal aerodromes. In the Second World War it repaired Lancaster bombers; its 18 concrete pillboxes are all listed. The airfield closed on 6 June 2025, sold to the Vistry Group to build 4,000 houses.

Wikipedia: Nottingham (Tollerton) Airport

Ruddington Depot

1940 · 1899

Ruddington's station on the Great Central Railway opened on 15 March 1899. From 1940 a depot was built here to fill and store ammunition, and it closed in 1983. The station had closed in 1963, and the Nottingham Heritage Railway now runs trains on the line. The rest of the depot's land is a business park and Rushcliffe Country Park.

The Lady Bay bombs

In 1941 a German bomber dropped a line of bombs across Lady Bay. New houses went up on the bomb sites in the 1950s and 1960s, and Pinders Ponds, to the east, are thought to be flooded bomb craters.

Wikipedia: Lady Bay, Nottinghamshire

The Nottingham Blitz, 1941

built in 1377

On the night of 8–9 May 1941 the Luftwaffe bombed Nottingham. Firefighters saved St Mary's Church after an incendiary burned through its roof; three Boots factories, in Poplar Street, Island Street and Station Street, were on fire; University College and the Shakespeare Street chapel were hit. The worst loss was at the Co-op bakery on Meadow Lane, where 49 people died. In all, 159 people were killed that night.

Wikipedia: Nottingham Blitz

Vera Anderson

1941 · Capital One

Vera Anderson, a Boots nurse, was on duty when bombs wrecked the Boots Printing Works here on 8 May 1941. She gave first aid to a dying night watchman through burning debris, then treated casualties at the control centre, and was awarded the George Medal.

War graves at St Wilfrid's

1943

The churchyard holds the war graves of eight soldiers of the First World War and an airman of the Second. Albert Harvey Iremonger, killed in action on 14 July 1943, aged 27, is buried here.
