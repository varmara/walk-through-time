Trent Bridge

built in 1868

The iron bridge was designed by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton and built between 1868 and 1871. A medieval bridge of more than 20 stone arches, begun in 1156, crossed the river nearby.

Victoria Embankment

A mile and a quarter of flood defences along the Trent, built between 1898 and 1901 under the borough engineer Arthur Brown. Part of it was later laid out as Memorial Gardens to the dead of the First World War.

Meadow Lane

Notts County moved here on 3 September 1910. Divided by the Trent, Meadow Lane and the City Ground are the closest pair of football grounds in England.

The Meadows rebuilt

Much of the Meadows' pre-1919 terraced housing was demolished to make way for a new development of modern council housing, and the viaduct carrying the Great Central railway came down in 1975.

City War Memorial

1927

Designed by the City Engineer Thomas Wallis Gordon, on land beside the Trent given by Sir Jesse Boot, and unveiled on 11 November 1927. It remembers the men of Nottingham who died in both World Wars.
