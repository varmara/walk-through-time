The Meadows

The Meadows, the low ground between the city and the Trent, was built over with terraced housing before the First World War. The area was protected by the Victoria Embankment, is home to Notts County's ground, and was largely rebuilt in the 1970s.

Trent Bridge

1156

In 1156 a stone bridge of more than 20 arches was begun here. It was known as the Hethbeth bridge, and two of its arches survive on the south bank. The present iron bridge nearby was designed by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton, built between 1868 and 1871, and widened to 80 feet in 1924–26.

The oldest professional club

1862

Notts County, called the oldest professional football club in the world, gives 1862 as its foundation, though it was formally organised on 7 December 1864. Its first recorded matches were at the Meadows Cricket Ground, its home until October 1877. It played at Trent Bridge from 1883, and since 1910 at Meadow Lane, on land leased from Nottingham Corporation. In 1894 it beat Bolton Wanderers 4–1 at Goodison Park, the first Second Division team to win the FA Cup. Late in 1901 a set of Notts-style kits sent to Turin gave Juventus their black and white stripes.

Lady Bay Bridge

1878

Built in 1878 to carry the Midland Railway's direct line from Nottingham to Melton Mowbray across the Trent. When the line was abandoned in 1968 the crossing was turned into a road bridge, finished in 1979.

Victoria Embankment

1898

Between 1898 and 1901 a mile and a quarter of flood defences was built along the Trent under the borough engineer Arthur Brown. Part of it was later laid out as Memorial Gardens to the dead of the First World War.

Statue of Robert Juckes Clifton

1883

Sir Robert Juckes Clifton, of the Clifton family across the river, was elected MP for Nottingham in 1861 and died of typhoid in 1869, aged 42. The Wilford Toll Bridge he had planned opened the next year, and in 1883 William Philip Smith's stone statue of him was put up at its northern end. It is listed Grade II.

Statue of Queen Victoria

1905

Albert Toft's stone statue of Queen Victoria was unveiled in the Market Square in 1905. In 1953 it was moved to the Memorial Gardens on Victoria Embankment, and in 2022 it was cleaned and repaired, its sceptre mended and missing fingers replaced. It is listed Grade II.

Wilford Suspension Bridge

1906

Opened in 1906 to carry water mains across the Trent to the reservoir on Wilford Hill, with a footway over them; it was designed by Arthur Brown of Elliott & Brown. It was restored in 1983 and again in 2008–2010.

Meadow Lane

Notts County moved here on 3 September 1910. Divided by the Trent, Meadow Lane and the City Ground are the closest pair of football grounds in England.

City War Memorial

1927

On 11 November 1927 a memorial designed by the City Engineer Thomas Wallis Gordon was unveiled on land beside the Trent given by Sir Jesse Boot. It remembers the men of Nottingham who died in both World Wars.

The 1947 flood

built in 1868

In 1947 the Trent flooded 3,000 properties, 86 factories and 28 miles of road in Nottingham, and the city's first flood defence was built that year. Photographs of West Bridgford under water are in Inspire Picture Archive. A new defence along 27 km of the river, from Sawley to Colwick, was finished in 2012.

The Meadows rebuilt

1970s

Much of the Meadows' pre-1919 terraced housing was demolished to make way for a new development of modern council housing, and the viaduct carrying the Great Central railway came down in 1975.
