Bulwell

Buleuuelle

1086

Domesday Book, in 1086, records Bulwell as the village of Buleuuelle. The name probably comes from a spring, the Bull Well, or from an Anglo-Saxon man called Bulla and his well.

Bulwell market

c. 1320

A local law of about 1320 said that only those with covered premises could trade, so Bulwell's market grew up with stalls that were covered while in use and opened out when not. The Market Place has kept much of its old shape.

Bulwell stone

16th century

Bulwell's limestone was quarried for building. Stretches of the wall round Wollaton Hall, built in the late 16th century, are of Bulwell stone.

Bulwell Hall

1770

The landowner John Newton built Bulwell Hall in 1770. It was sold at auction in 1864 to Samuel Thomas Cooper, and was pulled down in 1956; its grounds are now Bulwell Hall Park.

Gloves and bleach

c. 1840

Victorian Bulwell lived by the Leen. Glove-making, begun in about 1840, employed some 700 people until 1864, and by 1853 there were three bleachworks along the river.

St Mary's, Bulwell

1849

Storms in 1843 damaged the old church of St Mary beyond repair, and a new one was built on its site in 1849–50 to the designs of Henry Isaac Stevens. It is listed Grade II.

The Bogs

1872

The Bogs, the wet meadow by the Leen, were common land. When the lord of the manor tried to enclose them in 1872, about a thousand people marched on the land to claim it, and the Corporation later bought it for the town. The park won a Green Flag Award in 2004.
