Tilchestune

1086

Ilkeston was probably founded in the 6th century, and is said to take its name from a founder called Elch. Domesday Book, in 1086, calls it Tilchestune, held mostly by Gilbert de Ghent. Queen Victoria made it a borough in 1887.

Ilkeston Charter Fair

1252

Henry III granted Ilkeston its charter in 1252, and its fair is held in the Market Place. Two fairs were joined into one in 1888, and the Charter Fair has been held every October since.

Stanton ironworks

1780s

Iron-working at Stanton grew into a huge concern from the 1780s. By the mid-19th century the works made 20,000 tons of iron castings a year, and 2.5 million by 1905. From 1985 the French Saint-Gobain group ran it, and the last casting, in 2007, was an emotional day. The pin is approximate.

No station for fifty years

2017

Ilkeston once had three stations. The Town station closed to passengers in 1947, the North in 1964 and the Junction in January 1967, leaving the town without a station for fifty years. After a long campaign a new Ilkeston station was approved in 2013 and, delayed by flood works and great crested newts, opened on 2 April 2017. The pin is approximate.
