Chilwell

Chilwell began as a hamlet on the road to Ashby-de-la-Zouch, where Roman finds show earlier settlement. It grew into a suburb in the 20th century, and in the First World War it was the site of the National Shell Filling Factory, where an explosion killed 134 people in 1918.

Roman Chilwell

Roman times

Roman buildings, pottery and coins have been found in Chilwell. It began as a hamlet on the road from Nottingham to Ashby-de-la-Zouch, and is in the Domesday Book of 1086.

1903

Hall, manor and green

1906

The Charlton family, after whom the Charlton Arms is named, lived at Chilwell Hall, since pulled down. In 1906 Chilwell Manor Golf Club was laid out on land that had belonged to the manor, and the Manor House and Chilwell Green stood until 1965.

National Shell Filling Factory

Viscount Chetwynd built the factory from 1915 to fill shells with explosive; it filled some 19 million during the First World War. On 1 July 1918 eight tons of TNT exploded, killing 134 people, or 137 by another count. The site is now Chetwynd Barracks.

Chilwell's ordnance depot

1942

Soon after the First World War the shell factory's site became a depot and barracks for the Royal Army Ordnance Corps. A film shot on 9 August 1942 shows Churchill and Matilda tanks for Russia being loaded onto a train here. It was still a central ordnance depot after the Second World War. Its central vehicle kit store moved to Bicester in 1958, but the site went on as a general stores depot and vehicle workshop.
