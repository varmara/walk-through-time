Gotham

Gotham is best known for the tales of its 'Wise Men', the fools of Gotham, already told in the 15th century. Its name later gave New York the nickname 'Gotham'. The village also had its medieval church and, from the late 19th century, gypsum mining.

St Lawrence's, Gotham

13th century

St Lawrence's dates from the 13th century; it was restored in 1789 and repaired in 1869, and Reuben Bosworth made its tower clock in 1848 for £70. Among its memorials is one to John St Andrew and his wife, of 1625. It is listed Grade I.

The Wise Men of Gotham

15th century

The "fools of Gotham" were already mentioned in the fifteenth century, in the Towneley plays. A legend says that the villagers played the fool, even trying to fence in a cuckoo, so that King John would build his road and hunting lodge elsewhere. In 1807 Washington Irving called New York "Gotham", and Batman's Gotham City took the name.

Goat home to Gotham City

1807

Gotham means "goat home" in Old English. Washington Irving gave the name "Gotham" to New York in his Salmagundi Papers of 1807, and Bill Finger in turn named Batman's city Gotham City.

Gotham's gypsum

1899

Gypsum was dug from Gotham Hill and made into plaster. A branch line about two miles long, opened in March 1899, ran west from the Great Central main line to the works; it closed in the early 1960s and is now a footpath. The pin is approximate.

The Gotham tornado, 1984

On 2 August 1984 a tornado hit Gotham, damaging buildings but injuring no one.
