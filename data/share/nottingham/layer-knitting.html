Knitting

The stocking frame

In 1589 William Lee of Calverton invented the stocking frame, a machine that knitted. Legend has it that Elizabeth I refused him a patent. The trade grew here, and from 1728 Nottingham was one of its main centres.

Arkwright's first mill

Richard Arkwright's first cotton mill opened in Nottingham in 1768, its machines turned by horses. By 1772 it had four storeys and 300 workers.

The first Luddites

The Luddite movement began in Arnold on 11 March 1811. Across Nottinghamshire the frame-breakers went after the "wide" knitting frames used to make cheap goods.

Wikipedia: Luddites

Lowe's Silk Mill

During the Reform riots of 1831, Lowe's Silk Mill in Beeston was burnt on 11 October, the day after Nottingham Castle, and the same day the riots ceased.

Wikipedia: 1831 reform riots

Framework Knitters' Museum

c. 1840

Ruddington's framework knitters worked at hand frames in workshops behind their cottages. Workshops of about 1840 and workers' cottages of about 1829, all listed, now make up the Framework Knitters' Museum.

The Leavers lace machine

John Leavers lived here, at Canning Circus, where a plaque calls him the inventor of the Leavers lace machine, in 1813. He improved John Heathcoat's lace machine, and in 1821 emigrated to Grand-Couronne in Normandy.

High Pavement Chapel

built in 1876

Built in 1876 to designs by Stuart Colman for a congregation that goes back to 1690 and became Unitarian in 1802. Worship ended in 1982; the chapel then housed a lace museum and is now a Pitcher & Piano.
