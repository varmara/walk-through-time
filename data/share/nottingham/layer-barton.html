Barton in Fabis

Barton in Fabis is a small farming village on the south bank of the Trent. Its name records the beans once grown in its fields, and its medieval church and 17th-century dovecote reflect a long agricultural history, with a ferry linking it across the river.

Glebe Farm Roman villa

1st century

A Roman villa of the winged-corridor kind stood at Glebe Farm, lived in from the 1st to the 4th century. In 1858 a mosaic floor about 15 feet by 10 was found under the soil here, and charred wood suggested the house had burned. The Castle Museum turned the floor down as a gift in 1878, as moving it would have cost a few pounds. It was later lifted and taken to the rectory, and has since been lost.

St George's Church

14th century

A church was built here in the 14th century. Altered over the centuries, it was restored in 1877 and 1885, and it is listed Grade I.

Manor Farm dovecote

1677

In 1677 an octagonal red-brick dovecote was built at Manor Farm. It is listed Grade II.

Barton in the Beans

The village's older name was Barton in the Beans, after the beans grown here; fabis is Latin for beans. A ferry crossed the Trent from here to Attenborough from before 1774. Today Fairham, a new district, is being built in the parish beside Clifton.
