Barton in Fabis and Thrumpton

Two villages on the south bank of the Trent, upstream from Clifton: Barton, once Barton in the Beans, and Thrumpton, with its Jacobean hall.

Barton in the Beans

The village's older name was Barton in the Beans, after the beans grown here; fabis is Latin for beans. A ferry crossed the Trent from here to Attenborough from before 1774. Today Fairham, a new district, is being built in the parish beside Clifton.

Thrumpton Hall

1607–17

The Powdrells, a Roman Catholic family, lost their house here after the Babington Plot; part of it survives inside the hall, with a priest hole. The Pigots' rose-brick house around it was largely complete by 1617.

Wikipedia: Thrumpton Hall

The hall's Jacobean cantilevered staircase was carved from the estate's own timber, under John Webb, a pupil of Inigo Jones.

Wikipedia: Thrumpton Hall

The hall came to the Byrons when George Byron, 8th Baron Byron, married Lucy Wescomb in 1843, and they kept it for about a hundred years. Frederick, the 10th Baron, was the village's vicar and from 1917 its squire as well: its "squarson".

Wikipedia: Thrumpton Hall

George Fitzroy Seymour owned the estate from 1949 to 1994, and Miranda Seymour has owned it since.
