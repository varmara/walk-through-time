Inventions and discoveries

The stocking frame

In 1589 William Lee of Calverton invented the stocking frame, a machine that knitted. Legend has it that Elizabeth I refused him a patent. The trade grew here, and from 1728 Nottingham was one of its main centres.

Raleigh Street

In 1885 a small bicycle workshop opened in Raleigh Street, and it gave the company its name. Frank Bowden took it over in 1888, and by 1897 Raleigh had a purpose-built works of seven and a half acres on Faraday Road.

Building D10

built in 1932

Built in 1930–1932 to make Boots' liquid medicines, hence its nickname, the "Wets". Designed by the engineer Owen Williams, it is listed Grade I, the largest Grade I-listed structure in Britain.

Wikipedia: Boots Factory Site

Building D6

1935–38

Owen Williams's second building for Boots, of 1935–1938, made tablets and lozenges, hence its nickname, the "Drys". It too is listed Grade I.

Ibuprofen

Ibuprofen was first made at Boots in Nottingham in December 1961, by Stewart Adams's team. Their later laboratory, on Pennyfoot Street, is now BioCity, and in 2013 a Royal Society of Chemistry blue plaque marked the discovery.

The MRI scanner

Peter Mansfield came to the University of Nottingham in 1964 and developed magnetic resonance imaging here. Just before Christmas 1978 a full-body scanner was installed, and he made the first scan of a live patient. He shared the Nobel Prize in 2003 with Paul Lauterbur.

Water, coal and munitions

Thomas Hawksley

1866

The water engineer Thomas Hawksley was born at Arnot Hill House in 1807. His Trent Bridge waterworks, completed in 1831, gave Britain its first high-pressure constant supply, which kept dirty water out of the mains.

Wikipedia: Thomas Hawksley

Papplewick Pumping Station

Built in 1881–1884 to pump drinking water for Nottingham from deep wells, with two beam engines by James Watt & Co. and an engine house by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton, ornate with cast iron and stained glass. It pumped until 1969 and opened as a museum in 1976.

Wikipedia: Papplewick Pumping Station

National Shell Filling Factory

Viscount Chetwynd built the factory from 1915 to fill shells with explosive; it filled some 19 million during the First World War. On 1 July 1918 eight tons of TNT exploded, killing 134 people. The site is now Chetwynd Barracks.

Wikipedia: National Shell Filling Factory, Chilwell
