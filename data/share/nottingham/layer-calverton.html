Calverton

Calverton has two claims to a place in history. William Lee, who invented the stocking frame in 1589, came from here, and in 1952 a new colliery opened that quadrupled the village's population within a decade.

Roman Calverton

Roman times

Aerial photographs show traces of two Roman marching camps in a field north-east of the Oxton Road and Whinbush Lane crossroads: a smaller one of four acres inside a larger one of about twenty-six acres. Nothing shows above ground, and the site is a scheduled monument. Camps like these were dug by an army unit for an overnight stop. Two very similar hoards of Roman coins were later found in the village, less than three hundred yards apart: in June 1959, while foundations were dug for Manor Park Infants' School on Collyer Road, and in about April 1960, while a house was built in Crookdole Lane. The first was buried in an earthenware pot.

Calvretone

1086

The village appears as Calvretone in the Domesday survey of 1086, with a church and a priest. St Wilfrid's Church dates mostly from the 14th century; its nave and tower were rebuilt in 1760–63 and its chancel in 1835, and it was restored in 1881.

The stocking frame

1589

In 1589 William Lee of Calverton invented the stocking frame, a machine that knitted. Legend has it that Elizabeth I refused him a patent. The trade grew here, and from 1728 Nottingham was one of its main centres.

Calverton Colliery

1952

Calverton Colliery was officially opened on 24 September 1952, and the village grew from 1,304 people in 1951 to 5,658 in 1961. British Coal shut the mine on 19 November 1993; it worked again under RJB Mining until it closed for good in April 1999.
