Sherwood

A suburb along Mansfield Road: a Georgian house, a tram depot, and a bleacher's grange that became a park.

Woodthorpe House

c. 1750

Woodthorpe House, of about 1750, is the oldest building in Sherwood. It is now Sherwood Community Centre, at the north end of Mansfield Road opposite Woodthorpe Grange Park.

Samuel Hall's Sherwood

1825

Mansfield Road was the main road from London to Yorkshire, and a turnpike from 1787. Samuel Hall laid out Sherwood's first streets beside it in 1825; the road was paved and lit in 1879, the year horse trams arrived.

Woodthorpe Grange

1874

Henry Ashwell, a Basford bleacher, built Woodthorpe Grange in 1874. The council bought the house and grounds in 1921 for £15,000, Jesse Boot giving £5,000 of it, and the park opened in 1922.

Sherwood tram depot

1900

Nottingham Corporation Tramways built the depot in 1900 when it carried the Carrington tram line on up Mansfield Road to Winchester Street. Trams ran until 6 September 1936, when buses replaced them. Part of the building is now the Samuel Hall pub, named after the man who laid out Sherwood.

Wikipedia: Sherwood, Nottingham
