The flat land along the Trent east of the city: seven centuries of Pierreponts, their Tudor brick hall and church, a power station refused, and the gravel pits that became a centre for watersports.

The Pierreponts

1257

Pierrepont is Norman French for "stone bridge". In 1257 Sir Henry Pierrepont married Annora de Manvers, heir to Holme, and the family stayed for almost seven centuries: Charles I made Sir Robert Pierrepont Earl of Kingston-upon-Hull in 1628, and George I made Evelyn Pierrepont a duke in 1715. The estate was broken up and sold in 1940.

Holme Pierrepont Hall

c. 1280

The Pierreponts held Holme from about 1280. Around 1500 the hall was built, one of the few brick houses of its time in Nottinghamshire. The family became Earls and Dukes of Kingston, then Earls Manvers, and the hall is listed Grade I.

St Edmund's, Holme Pierrepont

1666

Henry Pierrepont, 1st Marquess of Dorchester, largely rebuilt the medieval church in 1666, and in 1878 T. C. Hine added its chancel. Its monuments remember the Pierreponts, from Sir Henry, who died in 1499, onwards, and the satirical poet John Oldham. It is listed Grade I.

The power station that was not built

1960

In 1960 the Central Electricity Generating Board proposed a 2,000 MW power station at Holme Pierrepont, with coal by rail and cooling water from the Trent. A public inquiry of 14 days turned it down, partly because it would have shut off the sand and gravel beneath. The gravel was dug instead, and in 1971 the pits became the National Watersports Centre.

National Watersports Centre

Dug out of a former gravel works, the centre opened in 1971, and hosted the World Rowing Championships in 1975 and 1986. Its whitewater course was built in 1986.
