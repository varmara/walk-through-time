St Laurence's, Long Eaton

12th century

Domesday Book calls the village Aitone. Its church of St Laurence stands east of the Market Place: local tradition dates parts of it to the 11th century and King Cnut, but its oldest parts may be from after the Conquest, in the 12th century. It was a daughter church of Sawley until the 19th century. It is listed Grade II*.

The Great Fire of Long Eaton

1694

In 1694 the "Great Fire" of Long Eaton destroyed 14 houses and several other buildings in the market place.

Long Eaton and the railway

1839

Long Eaton stayed a village until the Midland Counties Railway came in 1839. A great railway yard, Toton Sidings, grew up just north of the town, and by 1900 it had more than 10,000 people.

Long Eaton lace

1907

Lace made Long Eaton. High Street Mill dates from 1857, West End Mill of 1882 stands beside the Erewash Canal, and by 1907 the town had almost 1,400 lace machines and more than 4,000 people in the trade.

Harrington Mill

1885

Harrington Mill, one of the largest lace mills, was built in 1885. It took one and a quarter million bricks to build, and is 167 metres long.

Art Nouveau Long Eaton

1901

The town's new wealth shows in its Art Nouveau buildings: York Chambers of 1901 and the Midland Counties District Bank of 1903, at 38 and 40 Market Place, and the Carnegie Library on Tamworth Road of 1906, by the same architects, Gorman and Ross. York Chambers and the library are listed Grade II.
