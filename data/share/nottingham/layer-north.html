Nottingham General Hospital

1782

A legacy from the banker John Key paid for a hospital on part of Nottingham Park. Its first building, designed by John Simpson, was begun on 12 February 1781 and opened with 44 beds in September 1782; John Wesley was an early visitor. In 1844 it took in the hurt when a stand collapsed at a hanging on Garner's Hill, killing twelve. Hine added a storey in 1855, and Alfred Waterhouse's round Jubilee Wing opened in 1900. It closed in 1992, its work moved to the Queen's Medical Centre.

The Leavers lace machine

John Leavers lived here, at Canning Circus, where a plaque calls him the inventor of the Leavers lace machine, in 1813. He improved John Heathcoat's lace machine, and in 1821 emigrated to Grand-Couronne in Normandy.

General Cemetery

1836

In 1836 a cemetery of 18 acres was established at the top of Sion Hill, now Canning Circus, with a gatehouse and almshouses by Samuel Sutton Rawlinson. More than 150,000 people are buried here.

The Arboretum

1852

On 11 May 1852 a park laid out under the botanist Samuel Curtis opened here. Its bandstand went up in 1907. The bell from a temple in Canton, taken in the Second Opium War, hung in its war memorial until 1956.

The Arboretum war memorial

1862

The Arboretum's bell tower is a war memorial to the 59th (2nd Nottinghamshire) Regiment of Foot, designed by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton in 1862–63. Its cupola held a bell that British troops took from a temple in Canton during the Second Opium War, until the bell went to the regimental museum in 1956. Two of the cannon beside it were taken at Sebastopol in the Crimean War; the other two are copies.

Wikipedia: The Arboretum, Nottingham

Memorial to Samuel Morley

1928

Joseph Else's memorial to Samuel Morley, of bronze and stone, stands at the Arboretum's Waverley Street entrance: the hosier who was elected the town's MP in 1865. The list of the city's public art dates it to 1928. It is listed Grade II.

Votes for women, 1918

1918

The Representation of the People Act, passed on 6 February 1918, gave the vote to women of 30 and over who met a property qualification, and they voted for the first time on 14 December 1918. That November women had also been allowed to stand for Parliament: in Nottinghamshire the first to do so was Violet Markham, at Mansfield, who lost. In 1919 Bridgid Burke-Bloor became the first woman to stand for Nottingham City Council, and in 1920 Helena Dowson and Caroline Harper were its first women councillors. Women won the vote on the same terms as men on 2 July 1928. A hundred years to the day after that first vote, the city unveiled a plaque by a juniper tree in the Arboretum for the suffragette Helen Watts.

Guildhall

1888

Built in 1887–88 to designs by Thomas Verity and George Henry Hunt, chosen in a competition judged by Alfred Waterhouse, it replaced the old Guild Hall on Weekday Cross and served as the city's magistrates' court. The council used it as offices until 2010; it has stood largely empty since.

Nottingham Victoria station

built in 1899

Nottingham Victoria, the Great Central and Great Northern railways' station, opened on 24 May 1900 and closed on 4 September 1967. It was demolished except for its clock tower, which still stands at the entrance of the Victoria Centre built on the site.

George Brough

1919 · Brough Superior works, A building and 1 more

George Brough was a motorcycle racer and a world record–holding motorcycle and automobile manufacturer and showman. He was known for his powerful and expensive Brough Superior motorcycles which were the first superbikes. George died in 1970 but his legacy lives on in the many Brough Superior motorcycles maintained by enthusiasts to this day.
