Carlton

Carlton was a small township of farmers and framework knitters east of Nottingham. In the 19th century the textile trade and the railway made it a growing suburb, with a new church built by the Earl of Carnarvon and brickworks on the hill.

Carentune

1086

Domesday Book, in 1086, calls it Carentune. For centuries it was a small township in the manor and parish of Gedling, home to farmers and framework knitters.

A railway suburb

1840s

The textile trade and the railways grew Carlton in the 19th century: its population climbed steadily from the 1840s, and by 1850 it had become a suburb with its own houses, churches and schools. The tram line from Nottingham reached it in 1914.

Colwick Hall, 1831

built in 1831

On 10 October 1831, during the Reform riots, a crowd marched on Colwick Hall, the home of John Musters, and sacked and partly burned it. His wife, Mary Chaworth Musters, spent the night in pouring rain with her daughter Sophia, hiding beneath the shrubbery; she died at Wiverton Hall some four months later, from the shock. The hall is now a hotel.

Florence Paton's sculpture, 2019

2019

On International Women's Day, 8 March 2019, a stainless steel bust of Florence Paton, Nottinghamshire's first woman MP, was unveiled in the Garden for the Blind on Carlton Hill. She sat for Rushcliffe, which then took in Carlton, from 1945 to 1950, and pressed for safer mines and better health care for women and children. The sculptor Hilary Cartmel gave it miners' picks and a lamp for her ties to the area, books for her work in education, and the crowned portcullis of Parliament. The garden itself, of herbs and scented plants in raised beds, was made for people with little or no sight.
