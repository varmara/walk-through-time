Gedling

A village church with a famous spire, a colliery that became a country park, a closed station and the woods of Gedling House.

All Hallows Church, Gedling

13th century

All Hallows Church dates from the 13th century. It was restored in 1872, and its roof in 1892 by Arthur Blomfield. Its tall, thin broach spire has two tiers of lucarnes and a weathercock, and the church is listed Grade I.

Gedling Colliery

1899

Gedling Colliery opened in 1899 and was the area's main employer for almost a century. In the 1960s it dug over a million tonnes of coal a year with 1,400 men, about one in ten of them from the Caribbean, and was known as the "pit of all nations"; 128 men died there over its life. It closed in 1991, and in 2015 its grounds opened as Gedling Country Park.

Gedling station

1960

Gedling's railway station closed on 4 April 1960, though coal trains ran to the colliery for decades more. Its building is now a youth centre, and there are plans to reopen the line or make it a cycleway.

Gedling House Woods

1992

The woods and meadows of Gedling House are owned by the borough council and cared for by a friends' group. The woods became a local nature reserve in 1992, and the meadow joined them in 2007. The pin is approximate.
