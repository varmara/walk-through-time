The river and its bridges

A fort at the bridge

The settlement began in the early 10th century, when defences and houses were built at the south end of Trent Bridge to guard Nottingham against the Danes. Domesday, in 1086, records it as part of the manor of Clifton.

Wikipedia: West Bridgford

Trent Bridge

built in 1868

The iron bridge was designed by Marriott Ogle Tarbotton and built between 1868 and 1871. A medieval bridge of more than 20 stone arches, begun in 1156, crossed the river nearby.

Wikipedia: Trent Bridge (bridge)

Wilford Toll Bridge

built in 1870

Opened in 1870, replacing a ferry that had crossed here for some 400 years. Pedestrians paid a halfpenny toll. It was closed to traffic in 1974.

Wikipedia: Wilford Toll Bridge

Coal was found on the Clifton estate, and work on Clifton Colliery began in 1868. Sir Robert Juckes Clifton, the last of the Clifton baronets, died of typhoid in 1869; the colliery and this toll bridge, both his projects, opened in 1870.

Wikipedia: Robert Juckes Clifton

Victoria Embankment

A mile and a quarter of flood defences along the Trent, built between 1898 and 1901 under the borough engineer Arthur Brown. Part of it was later laid out as Memorial Gardens to the dead of the First World War.

Wikipedia: The Meadows, Nottingham

Wilford Suspension Bridge

built in 1907

A footbridge built by the city's water department, mainly to carry water pipes across the Trent to the reservoir on Wilford Hill. The engineer was Arthur Brown.

Wikipedia: Wilford Suspension Bridge

Clifton Bridge

built in 1972

Opened in 1958 to take traffic off Trent Bridge, it was then the longest pre-stressed concrete bridge in the country. A second bridge beside it, opened in 1972, made the crossing a dual carriageway.

Wikipedia: Clifton Bridge (Nottingham)

Lady Bay Bridge

The Midland Railway built this bridge for its direct line to Melton Mowbray and London. The line closed in 1969, and in 1979 the bridge was turned over to road traffic.

Canal and railways

The Nottingham Canal

built in 1895

Authorised in 1792 and engineered by William Jessop, the canal opened in 1796 from the Trent to Langley Mill, bringing coal to the town. Railways took its trade; most of the main line was abandoned in 1937.

Wikipedia: Nottingham Canal

The railway that began in a pub

In 1832 coal owners meeting at the Sun Inn, Eastwood, proposed extending the Mansfield and Pinxton Railway to Leicester. It became the Midland Counties Railway, whose line from Nottingham, at Carrington Street, to Derby opened in 1839.

Wikipedia: Midland Counties Railway

Edwalton station

The station opened in 1880 on the Midland Railway's direct line from Nottingham to Melton Mowbray, which let its London expresses avoid Leicester. It closed to passengers in 1941, and its site is now housing. Beyond it the line became the Old Dalby test track, where the Advanced Passenger Train was tried.

Wikipedia: Edwalton railway station

Bicycles, buses and aircraft

Thomas Humber

Plaque to Thomas Humber

Thomas Humber was an English engineer and cycle manufacturer who developed and patented a safety bicycle with a diamond-shaped frame and wheels of similar size. It became a pattern for subsequent machines. Humber made many other improvements to bicycles.

Wikipedia: Thomas Humber

Raleigh Street

In 1885 a small bicycle workshop opened in Raleigh Street, and it gave the company its name. Frank Bowden took it over in 1888, and by 1897 Raleigh had a purpose-built works of seven and a half acres on Faraday Road.

Wikipedia: Raleigh Bicycle Company

Tollerton airfield

1941

Licensed in 1929 and opened in 1930 by Sir Sefton Brancker, it was one of Britain's first municipal aerodromes. In the Second World War it repaired Lancaster bombers; its 18 concrete pillboxes are all listed.

Wikipedia: Nottingham (Tollerton) Airport
