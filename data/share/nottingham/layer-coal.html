Coal and power

The coalfield round Nottingham: the pits, the wagonway, canal and railways that carried the coal, and the power stations by the Trent that burned it.

The pits

Sir Robert Clifton's colliery

built in 1870

Opened in 1870, replacing a ferry that had crossed here for some 400 years. Pedestrians paid a halfpenny toll. It was closed to traffic in 1974.

Wikipedia: Wilford Toll Bridge

Coal was found on the Clifton estate, and work on Clifton Colliery began in 1868. Sir Robert Juckes Clifton, the last of the Clifton baronets, died of typhoid in 1869; the colliery and this toll bridge, both his projects, opened in 1870.

Wikipedia: Robert Juckes Clifton

Bestwood winding engine

1873

The winding engine house of Bestwood Colliery, worked by the Bestwood Coal and Iron Company, keeps its vertical steam engine of 1873, which lowered the miners and wound up the coal. The pit closed in 1967.

Cotgrave Colliery

The colliery came in the early 1960s: from 1962 hundreds of mining families moved here from Radford's closed pit, and the village grew several times over. The pit worked through the 1984–85 strike and closed in 1993.

Carrying the coal

Wollaton Wagonway

In 1603–1604 Huntingdon Beaumont and Sir Percival Willoughby laid a wooden wagonway from the Strelley coal pits to Wollaton Lane End, its wagons drawn by horses. It is described as the first railway in Britain.

Wikipedia: Wollaton Wagonway

The Nottingham Canal

built in 1895

Authorised in 1792 and engineered by William Jessop, the canal opened in 1796 from the Trent to Langley Mill, bringing coal to the town. Railways took its trade; most of the main line was abandoned in 1937.

Wikipedia: Nottingham Canal

The railway that began in a pub

In 1832 coal owners meeting at the Sun Inn, Eastwood, proposed extending the Mansfield and Pinxton Railway to Leicester. It became the Midland Counties Railway, whose line from Nottingham, at Carrington Street, to Derby opened in 1839.

Wikipedia: Midland Counties Railway

Bennerley Viaduct

built in 1877

Built in 1876–1877 of wrought iron, light enough for ground undermined by coal workings. It closed in 1968, was too costly to take down, and reopened to walkers and cyclists in 2022; only Meldon Viaduct in Devon is like it.

Wikipedia: Bennerley Viaduct

Power stations

Wilford Power Station

A coal-fired power station on the north bank of the Trent, opened in 1925. It closed in 1981 and was soon demolished; the Riverside Retail Park stands on its site.

Wikipedia: Wilford Power Station

Show on the map

Ratcliffe-on-Soar Power Station

Generating from 1968, with eight cooling towers, it was the last coal-fired power station in Britain. When it shut in September 2024 it ended 142 years of coal power in the country.

Wikipedia: Ratcliffe-on-Soar Power Station
