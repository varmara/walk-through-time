The industrial revolution

How Nottingham and its coalfield helped make the industrial age: an early railway of wood, the first mill of the factory system, the canal and the railway that carried the coal, the lace machine, the frame-breakers who fought the machines, and the iron and steam that outlasted the pits.

Textiles and the machines

Arkwright's first mill

Richard Arkwright's first cotton mill opened in Nottingham in 1768, its machines turned by horses. By 1772 it had four storeys and 300 workers.

The first Luddites

The Luddite movement began in Arnold on 11 March 1811. Across Nottinghamshire the frame-breakers went after the "wide" knitting frames used to make cheap goods.

The Leavers lace machine

John Leavers lived here, at Canning Circus, where a plaque calls him the inventor of the Leavers lace machine, in 1813. He improved John Heathcoat's lace machine, and in 1821 emigrated to Grand-Couronne in Normandy.

Lowe's Silk Mill

During the Reform riots of 1831, Lowe's Silk Mill in Beeston was burnt on 11 October, the day after Nottingham Castle, and the same day the riots ceased.

Coal, canals and railways

Wollaton Wagonway

In 1603–1604 Huntingdon Beaumont and Sir Percival Willoughby laid a wooden wagonway from the Strelley coal pits to Wollaton Lane End, its wagons drawn by horses. It is described as the first railway in Britain.

Wikipedia: Wollaton Wagonway

The Nottingham Canal

built in 1895

Authorised in 1792 and engineered by William Jessop, the canal opened in 1796 from the Trent to Langley Mill, bringing coal to the town. Railways took its trade; most of the main line was abandoned in 1937.

Wikipedia: Nottingham Canal

The railway that began in a pub

In 1832 coal owners meeting at the Sun Inn, Eastwood, proposed extending the Mansfield and Pinxton Railway to Leicester. It became the Midland Counties Railway, whose line from Nottingham, at Carrington Street, to Derby opened in 1839.

Wikipedia: Midland Counties Railway

Bestwood winding engine

1873

The winding engine house of Bestwood Colliery, worked by the Bestwood Coal and Iron Company, keeps its vertical steam engine of 1873, which lowered the miners and wound up the coal. The pit closed in 1967.

Wikipedia: Bestwood Country Park

Bennerley Viaduct

built in 1877

Built in 1876–1877 of wrought iron, light enough for ground undermined by coal workings. It closed in 1968, was too costly to take down, and reopened to walkers and cyclists in 2022; only Meldon Viaduct in Devon is like it.

Wikipedia: Bennerley Viaduct
