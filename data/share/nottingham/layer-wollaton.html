Wollaton

Wollaton was the village of the Willoughby family, who also worked coal here. Their Elizabethan hall, built in the 1580s, still dominates the park, and as early as 1603–04 a wooden wagonway carried coal from their pits. The last colliery here closed in 1965.

St Leonard's, Wollaton

13th century

The old village of Wollaton gathers round two buildings: the church of St Leonard, which goes back to the 13th century, and the Admiral Rodney pub. The church is listed Grade II*.

Wollaton dovecote

c. 1565

The Willoughbys built the village dovecote in about 1565. It is one of Wollaton's Elizabethan survivals, with its old cottages and water pump, and is now a small museum.

Wollaton Hall

1580

Sir Francis Willoughby built Wollaton Hall between 1580 and 1588, probably to designs by the Elizabethan architect Robert Smythson. After a fire in 1642 the house stood empty for some forty years; Wyatville remodelled it from 1801. The 11th Baron Middleton sold it to Nottingham Corporation for £200,000, and since it opened to the public in 1926 it has held the city's natural history museum.

Wollaton Wagonway

In 1603–1604 Huntingdon Beaumont and Sir Percival Willoughby laid a wooden wagonway from the Strelley coal pits to Wollaton Lane End, its wagons drawn by horses. It is described as the first railway in Britain.

Wollaton Colliery

1965

Coal has always mattered in Wollaton, from the wagonway onwards. Wollaton Colliery closed in 1965, and houses now cover its ground and that of the old canal: the Torvill and Dean estate.
