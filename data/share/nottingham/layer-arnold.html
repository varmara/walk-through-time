Ernehale

1086

Domesday Book, in 1086, calls the village Ernehale. A history of Arnold written in 1913 traces the name back to its Anglo-Saxon settlers, worn down over the centuries into Arnold.

Arnot Hill House

1791

The mill owner John Hawksley built a large cotton mill here, working before the end of 1791 with a 60-horsepower engine, and laid out Arnot Hill Park in about 1792 as a setting for his house. His son Thomas Hawksley, born at Arnot Hill House in 1807, became the engineer who gave Nottingham clean water. The house is listed Grade II.

Richard Parkes Bonington

Bonington House

Richard Parkes Bonington was an English Romantic landscape painter, who in his last years also painted many historical figure paintings. His family moved to France when he was 14 and he can also be considered as a French artist, and an intermediary bringing aspects of English style to France.

Thomas Hawksley

1866

The water engineer Thomas Hawksley was born at Arnot Hill House in 1807. His Trent Bridge waterworks, completed in 1831, gave Britain its first high-pressure constant supply, which kept dirty water out of the mains.

The first Luddites

The Luddite movement began in Arnold on 11 March 1811. Across Nottinghamshire the frame-breakers went after the "wide" knitting frames used to make cheap goods.

Home Brewery

1875

John Robinson founded the Home Brewery in 1875; its beer mats carried a Robin Hood badge. The family sold it in 1986, and its closure cost some 400 jobs. Its building of 1936 is now Sir John Robinson House, and listed Grade II.

Wikipedia: Arnold, Nottinghamshire

Arnold Market

1968

Arnold was a town of hosiery factories, and in 1968 it gained its own market in the town centre.
