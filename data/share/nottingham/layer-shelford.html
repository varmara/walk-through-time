Shelford's church and priory

1310

By 1310–11 Shelford's church belonged to an Augustinian priory here. After Henry VIII dissolved the monasteries, the Stanhopes acquired the priory, in 1537, and with it the church. Lady Anne Stanhope's tomb, by Gabriel Royle, was put up after her death in 1588, and the family vault is dated 1677. In 1877–78 Lord Carnarvon paid for Ewan Christian to restore the church.

The storming of Shelford House

In November 1645 Parliament's troops under Sydnam Poyntz, with Colonel John Hutchinson leading the assault, stormed Shelford House, held for the King by Philip Stanhope. About 160 of its defenders were killed, and the house was burnt that night.

The siege in the tower

1645

When Parliament's forces took Shelford House on 3 November 1645, Royalists led by a woman fired on them from the church tower; John Hutchinson had straw set alight to smoke them out, and smoke stains in the tower stairwell may date from then. Lady Anne Stanhope's monument and the font were damaged; the font was replaced in 1662.
