Jesse Boot

Plaque to Jesse Boot, Dorothy Boot Homes

Jesse Boot, 1st Baron Trent transformed The Boots Company, founded by his father, John Boot, into a national retailer, which branded itself as "Chemists to the Nation".

Louisa May Long

Florence Boot Hall

Louisa May Long (1892–1981), who nursed with the 94 Nottingham VAD at Radcliffe on Trent in the First World War, was Matron of Florence Boot Hall from 1928 to 1946.

City War Memorial

1927

Designed by the City Engineer Thomas Wallis Gordon, on land beside the Trent given by Sir Jesse Boot, and unveiled on 11 November 1927. It remembers the men of Nottingham who died in both World Wars.

Wikipedia: City War Memorial, Nottingham

Building D10

built in 1932

Built in 1930–1932 to make Boots' liquid medicines, hence its nickname, the "Wets". Designed by the engineer Owen Williams, it is listed Grade I, the largest Grade I-listed structure in Britain.

Wikipedia: Boots Factory Site

Building D6

1935–38

Owen Williams's second building for Boots, of 1935–1938, made tablets and lozenges, hence its nickname, the "Drys". It too is listed Grade I.

Wikipedia: Boots Factory Site

Vera Anderson

Capital One

Vera Anderson, a Boots nurse, was on duty when bombs wrecked the Boots Printing Works here on 8 May 1941. She gave first aid to a dying night watchman through burning debris, then treated casualties at the control centre, and was awarded the George Medal.

Ibuprofen

Ibuprofen was first made at Boots in Nottingham in December 1961, by Stewart Adams's team. Their later laboratory, on Pennyfoot Street, is now BioCity, and in 2013 a Royal Society of Chemistry blue plaque marked the discovery.
