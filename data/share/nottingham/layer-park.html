The castle's deer park

1663

The Park began as a wooded deer park on the west side of Nottingham Castle, hunted while the castle belonged to the Crown. In 1663 William Cavendish, 1st Duke of Newcastle, bought the ruined castle together with its park, and the land stayed with his successors for almost three centuries.

Park Terrace

1829

The first house in the park, built in 1809 opposite the castle gatehouse, was the vicarage of St Mary's. In 1827 the architect Peter Frederick Robinson published a plan for building over the park, and houses began to go up along Park Terrace in about 1829; within three years there were 40 to 50 of them.

Tower House

1827

Tower House is one of the earliest houses in the Park, built to P. F. Robinson's designs between 1827 and 1833. Watson Fothergill later gave it the tower that names it. It is listed Grade II.

Hine's Park

1854

In 1854 the Duke's estate took on the Nottingham architect Thomas Chambers Hine, to design many of the Park's villas. The roads are still lit by gas lamps, believed to be one of the largest such networks left in Europe.

The Park Tunnel

1855

The tunnel was meant to let carriages from the Park reach Derby Road through the sandstone. It was begun in about 1844 and finished by T. C. Hine in 1855–56, but at 1 in 12 it was too steep for horses and carriages, so it became a footway. It runs from Tunnel Road to Derby Road, with steps halfway up to Upper College Street.

Who lived in the Park

The Park's villas housed the town's business and public figures: the publisher Richard Allen, who lived at Albert Villa until his death in 1884; the MP Anthony Mundella, for whom Amelia House was built; and Captain Athelstan Popkess, chief constable of Nottingham from 1930 to 1959, at 35 Newcastle Drive.

Sold, saved and run by its residents

1938

The 8th Duke of Newcastle sold the Park in 1938 to the Nuffield Trust, which passed it on to Oxford University. The estate became a conservation area in 1969, and talks that began in 1986 between the residents' association and the university put the estate in the hands of its residents, who run it from the Estate Office.
