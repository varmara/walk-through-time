Castle Marina

The Nottingham Canal, opened in 1796, brought coal and goods into the town, and wharves and warehouses grew up along it west of the centre. When the canal trade declined, the old railway sidings beside it were dug out in the 1980s for a marina.

The Nottingham Canal

built in 1895

Authorised in 1792 and engineered by William Jessop, the canal opened in 1796 from the Trent to Langley Mill, bringing coal to the town. Railways took its trade; most of the main line was abandoned in 1937.

Fellows Morton & Clayton

1895

Fellows Morton & Clayton, who ran the largest fleet of barges and narrowboats in the country, built this canalside depot in 1895. Boats loaded and unloaded at the wharf beside it; the building is now a pub, and listed Grade II.

The Trent Navigation warehouse

c. 1919

The Trent Navigation Company put up this warehouse at Castle Wharf in about 1919. It later became British Waterways' offices, was converted as part of the Castle Wharf scheme in 1996, and in 2022 was given permission for 95 flats. Which building it was is not certain.

The marina on the sidings

1980s

West of the town the canal ran past railway sidings. In the 1980s the sidings were dug out for Castle Marina, a basin of moorings off the canal, with shops and offices round it.
