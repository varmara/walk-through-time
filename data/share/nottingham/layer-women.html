Helena Dowson

Nottingham Magistrates' Court

Helena Dowson ran Nottingham's suffrage society from 1895 and its shop on Long Row. In 1920 she became the first woman magistrate and a Liberal councillor for the Meadows; a plaque here at the Justice Centre remembers her.

Helen Watts

Albert Hall

Helen Watts, the daughter of the vicar of Lenton, joined Nottingham's suffragettes after hearing Christabel Pankhurst speak in 1907. In 1909 she served a month in Holloway, was arrested here at the Albert Hall in July and let go without charge, and went on hunger strike in Leicester gaol.

Nellie Crocker

The suffragettes' office, Carlton Street

Nellie Crocker was the suffragettes' organiser in Nottingham from 1909 to 1912, with an office on Carlton Street. After her eighth arrest, for attacking a post office in London, she spent three months of 1912 in Holloway.

Ann Morley

The People's Hall

Ann Morley, a businesswoman, built this house in 1750, a rare Georgian town house built by a woman. The Nottingham School of Design opened here in 1843, and in 1854 the lace merchant George Gill made it the People's Hall, an institute for working men.

Louisa Clarke

A building

Louisa Clarke opened a day and boarding school for girls in 1874, at 9 Park Terrace; called Western House, it moved here, to 30 The Ropewalk, in 1890. Rose Feilmann, who wrote "There are fairies at the bottom of my garden", was one of her pupils.

Laura Knight

Plaque to Laura Knight

Dame Laura Knight was an English artist who worked in oils, watercolours, etching, engraving and drypoint. Knight was a painter in the figurative, realist tradition, who embraced English Impressionism. In her long career, Knight was among the most successful and popular painters in Britain.

Margaret Glen-Bott

15 Regent Street

Margaret Glen-Bott (1891–1969) came to Nottingham in 1916. The first woman gynaecologist to be Honorary Surgeon at the Women's Hospital and the Children's Hospital, and later a magistrate, she lived at 15 Regent Street.

Sarah Gray

21 Regent Street

Sarah Gray, Nottingham's first woman GP, lived and practised at 21 Regent Street. At the Women's Hospital from 1899 she was refused charge of in-patients; in 1921 she became the first president of the Nottingham Medico-Chirurgical Society.

Mary Kendrick

Curzon Street

Mary Patricia Kendrick MBE born Mary Patricia Boak was a British tidal engineer who was an expert on silt. She worked on many projects but she is known for leading a team working on the Thames Barrier. She broke a 200 year long list of Admirals who looked after keeping the River Mersey navigable when she appointed Acting Conservator of the River Mersey - a role that dates back to 1625.

Wikipedia: Mary Kendrick

Mary Kendrick, the tidal engineer who led the studies of river levels and silt for the Thames Barrier, grew up on Curzon Street, where her father was a haulage contractor. She later kept the Mersey navigable as its Acting Conservator.

Vera Anderson

Capital One

Vera Anderson, a Boots nurse, was on duty when bombs wrecked the Boots Printing Works here on 8 May 1941. She gave first aid to a dying night watchman through burning debris, then treated casualties at the control centre, and was awarded the George Medal.
