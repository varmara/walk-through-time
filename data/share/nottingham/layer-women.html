Helen Watts

Albert Hall

Helen Kirkpatrick Watts was a militant British suffragette from Nottingham.

Wikipedia: Helen Kirkpatrick Watts

Helen Watts, the daughter of the vicar of Lenton, joined Nottingham's suffragettes after hearing Christabel Pankhurst speak in 1907. In 1909 she served a month in Holloway, was arrested here at the Albert Hall in July and let go without charge, and went on hunger strike in Leicester gaol.

Lucy Hutchinson

Owthorpe

Lucy Hutchinson was an English translator, poet, and biographer, and the first person to translate the complete text of Lucretius's De rerum natura into English verse, during the years of the Interregnum.

Wikipedia: Lucy Hutchinson

Lucy Hutchinson, born in the Tower of London in 1620, was the first to translate the whole of Lucretius into English verse, and her Order and Disorder is perhaps the first epic poem by a woman in English. Her life of her husband, John Hutchinson, written for her family, was printed in 1806. She died at Owthorpe in 1681.

George Wells Almshouses

1709

Their datestone reads "George Wells, Anno 1709", though Wells's will was written in 1712. The almshouses were for six poor unmarried or widowed women, kept up by his lands in Cropwell Bishop, Wilford and Ruddington, with Sir Gervase Clifton and his heirs as trustees; each woman had an allowance and three tons of coal a year from Clifton Colliery. Restored in the 1970s, they are now two homes.

Ann Morley

The People's Hall

Ann Morley, a businesswoman, built this house in 1750, a rare Georgian town house built by a woman. The Nottingham School of Design opened here in 1843, and in 1854 the lace merchant George Gill made it the People's Hall, an institute for working men.

Mary Howitt

Howitt House, South Parade

Mary Howitt was an English writer, editor, translator and a pioneer of the women's rights movement in the UK. She is most known as the author of the famous poem The Spider and the Fly. Howitt translated several works by Hans Christian Andersen and Frederika Bremer.

Wikipedia: Mary Howitt

Mary Howitt married William Howitt in 1821, and by 1823 they were living in Nottingham, on Timber Hill, now South Parade, next to Smith's Bank, for over twelve years. She wrote The Spider and the Fly and translated Hans Christian Andersen's tales.

Florence Boot

Florence Boot Hall

Florence Anne Boot, Lady Trent was a Jersey businesswoman and philanthropist. She assisted her husband, Jesse Boot, in running Boots chemists after their marriage in 1886. Florence was responsible for diversifying the firm's retail offering to include perfume, cosmetics, stationery, books, and other general merchandise and also pioneered in-store cafés.

Wikipedia: Florence Boot

Florence Boot, born in St Helier, Jersey, in 1863, married Jesse Boot in 1886 and helped him run the firm. She brought books and stationery into the Goose Gate shop and started Boots Book-Lovers' Library and cafés in the stores. She founded the university's first hall for women, named Florence Boot Hall after her.

Wikipedia: Florence Boot

Louisa May Long (1892–1981), who nursed with the 94 Nottingham VAD at Radcliffe on Trent in the First World War, was Matron of Florence Boot Hall from 1928 to 1946.

Louisa Clarke

A building

Jessie Batten, a London graduate of 1892, owned Western House school here from 1895. She married in 1910 but kept her own name at work, and retired in 1918.

Laura Knight

Plaque to Laura Knight

Dame Laura Knight was an English artist who worked in oils, watercolours, etching, engraving and drypoint. Knight was a painter in the figurative, realist tradition, who embraced English Impressionism. In her long career, Knight was among the most successful and popular painters in Britain.

Mary Potter

Hyson Green

The Little Company of Mary, also known as the Blue Sisters, is a Catholic religious institute of women dedicated to caring for the suffering, the sick, and the dying. The order was founded in 1877 in Nottingham, England by Mary Potter.

Wikipedia: Little Company of Mary

In 1877 Mary Potter's Little Company of Mary began in an abandoned factory in Hyson Green. The sisters' main work was caring for the sick and dying. The pin is approximate.

Sarah Gray

21 Regent Street

Sarah Gray, Nottingham's first woman GP, lived and practised at 21 Regent Street. At the Women's Hospital from 1899 she was refused charge of in-patients; in 1921 she became the first president of the Nottingham Medico-Chirurgical Society.

Mary Kendrick

Curzon Street

Mary Patricia Kendrick MBE born Mary Patricia Boak was a British tidal engineer who was an expert on silt. She worked on many projects but she is known for leading a team working on the Thames Barrier. She broke a 200 year long list of Admirals who looked after keeping the River Mersey navigable when she appointed Acting Conservator of the River Mersey - a role that dates back to 1625.

Wikipedia: Mary Kendrick

Mary Kendrick, the tidal engineer who led the studies of river levels and silt for the Thames Barrier, grew up on Curzon Street, where her father was a haulage contractor. She later kept the Mersey navigable as its Acting Conservator.

Wikipedia: Mary Kendrick

Vera Anderson

Capital One

Vera Anderson, a Boots nurse, was on duty when bombs wrecked the Boots Printing Works here on 8 May 1941. She gave first aid to a dying night watchman through burning debris, then treated casualties at the control centre, and was awarded the George Medal.
