St Wilfrid's Church

Late 14th century

The church dates from the late 14th century and is thought to have been founded by Gervase de Wilford in around 1361. Inside are two memorials to the poet Henry Kirke White, who drew much inspiration from Wilford and Clifton.

Wikipedia: St Wilfrid's Church, Wilford

Henry Kirke White is said to have spent much of his time writing poems in the churchyard, sitting under his favourite tree.

Chapels and preachers

John Wesley

Wesley Place Methodist Chapel, Hockley Methodist Chapel

John Wesley was an English cleric, theologian, and evangelist who was a principal leader of a revival movement within the Church of England known as Methodism. The societies he founded became the dominant form of the ongoing independent Methodist movement. Educated at Charterhouse and Christ Church, Oxford, Wesley was elected a fellow of Lincoln College, Oxford, in 1726 and ordained as an Anglican priest two years later.

Wikipedia: John Wesley

Plaque by Southern Broxtowe Blue Plaque Group: “Wesley Place Methodist Chapel. Built in 1782 near the spot where John Wesley preached in 1774 and 1780. Enlarged 1848. Closed for worship 1975.”

William Booth

William Booth's birthplace, Plaque to William Booth

William Booth was an English Methodist preacher who, along with his wife, Catherine, founded the Salvation Army and became its first General. This Christian movement, founded in 1865, has a quasi-military structure and government and has spread from London to many parts of the world. It is one of the largest distributors of humanitarian aid.

Wikipedia: William Booth

William Booth, who founded the Salvation Army, was born in this house in Notintone Place, Sneinton. It is now the William Booth Birthplace Museum, telling the story of Booth, his wife Catherine and their family.

The Salvation Army: William Booth Birthplace Museum

Red plaque: “In this house was born on 10th April 1829 William Booth founder and General of The Salvation Army”
